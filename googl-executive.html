Valuation — the methods disagree, and that matters

Why the market is wrong — three non-consensus points

  1. It is not the cheap megacap. The ~17× rests on $77.1bn of after-tax unrealised gains. Alphabet disclosed the reconciliation itself: $6.26 of the $9.11 reported Q2 EPS was paper, leaving $2.85 — which matches the $2.88 consensus almost exactly. Clean, the multiple is ~29.8×, at the peer median. Phase 4 — Alphabet's own Q2 2026 disclosure.
  2. The capex is compensated, not speculative. Alphabet is the only hyperscaler that can already show where the money converts: Cloud +82% with operating margin 20.7% → 35.6%, behind $514bn of contracted backlog. The bottleneck is capacity, not customers. Phases 2–3 — Q2 2026 Cloud segment disclosures.
  3. Antitrust resolved, and resolved well. No Chrome divestiture, no Android breakup, and on 2 September 2026 all three DOJ structural remedies in the ad tech case were rejected. Conduct regulation and an appeal remain — dismemberment does not. Phase 1 — Mehta remedy terms; Brinkema ruling 2026-09-02.

What would make this call wrong

  • The rating is 0.7pp from flipping. Fair value of $302 against $338.72 is −10.7%; the HOLD/AVOID boundary is −10%. A 100bp WACC change moves fair value ~15%, so this sits well inside the error bars.
  • The depreciation assumption is unverifiable from outside. ~60% of a $200bn programme is short-lived compute. Six-year to four-year lives would cut reported earnings by tens of billions — and it sits under the two methods carrying 80% of the weight.
  • Nothing in the operating picture supports a bear case. Revenue accelerated from 15% to ~21%, Search grew 17% against two years of disruption predictions, and Cloud compounds at 82% with expanding margin. This is a price call on a business that is firing.
  • The capex cycle has no guided peak. FY2027 consensus is ~$257bn, 28% above an already-doubled 2026, and the whole sector is committing simultaneously past $700bn.
