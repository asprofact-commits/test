What would make this call wrong

Stated before the case for it, because it is genuinely strong.

  • Conventional analysis has been wrong about this company for twenty years. Every conservative valuation of SpaceX since 2012 has been too low. A method giving zero weight to unproven capability will systematically undervalue a company whose defining characteristic is delivering unproven capability.
  • Starlink is genuinely superb — 12m subscribers doubling year on year, ~85% recurring, ~63% EBITDA margin — and is valued here at 32× EBITDA. At 40–50m subscribers the anchor used here is far too low.
  • The evidence base is unusually thin. Three months public, two quarters reported, no primary filing readable, net debt assumed zero and the share count derived. The honest error bar is wide in both directions.
  • The bull case is not absurd. If the $100bn target is delivered and sustained at high margin, the price is roughly fair rather than excessive — which is exactly why the Q3 report matters so much.

Why the market is wrong — three non-consensus points

  1. Quantify the optionality and it has to carry $1.05 trillion. That is the gap between a generous $875bn sum-of-the-parts — Starlink at 32× EBITDA, xAI above its own merger mark, launch at 11× revenue — and the $1.92tn price. More than the market cap of all but a handful of companies on earth, for assets not yet commercially operating. Phase 5 SOTP build; Phase 2 — Starship pre-commercial.
  2. The $100bn target is not upside to the price; it is the price. It requires ~3× the run rate in two quarters with no disclosed bridge — and the reverse valuation shows the current price already requires it, delivered and sustained at Starlink-like group margins. Phase 3 — Q2 revenue $7.8bn, ~$31bn annualised.
  3. The supply overhang is ahead, not behind. 911.5m shares unlocked in August; ~12.4bn more by 8 December, against a 638.9m IPO float. All price discovery so far has happened on under 5% of the company. Phase 4 — lockup schedule.
