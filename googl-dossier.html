The finding that changes the dossier

Alphabet is widely described as the cheapest megacap at roughly 17× earnings. That multiple is computed on trailing earnings containing $77.1bn of after-tax unrealised gains on SpaceX and one private holding. Alphabet disclosed the reconciliation itself.

Valuation football field

Unlike the Apple dossier, the methods disagree sharply — and the disagreement is informative. The DCF is the outlier and the least reliable instrument for a company two years into a capex cycle, so it carries 20% rather than the default 45%.

The capex cycle, and where it converts

An asset-light software company became an infrastructure business in eighteen months. What separates Alphabet from the rest of the AI capex cohort is that it can already point to the line where the money is converting.

$514bn of contracted backlog. More than four times total company revenue and roughly nine times annualised Cloud revenue. Most AI capex stories rest on speculative demand; Alphabet's rests on a contracted order book. The bottleneck is capacity, not customers — which justifies the spending, and also means it cannot stop while the backlog is unbuilt.

Why the market is mispricing this

  1. Market believes: Alphabet is the cheapest megacap at ~17× earnings.
    We believe: That multiple rests on $77.1bn of after-tax unrealised gains. Clean, it is ~29.8× — at the peer median. The single most common reason given for owning this stock does not survive checking. Evidence — Phase 4: Alphabet's own reconciliation of the $9.11 reported EPS.
  2. Market believes: The AI capex boom threatens Alphabet's cash generation.
    We believe: Alphabet is the only hyperscaler that can already show where the money converts — Cloud +82% with margin 20.7% → 35.6%, behind $514bn of contracted backlog. A compensated build against contracted demand, which is why the bottleneck is capacity rather than customers. Evidence — Phases 2–3: Q2 2026 Cloud segment disclosures and the backlog figure.
  3. Market believes: Antitrust is an unquantifiable overhang.
    We believe: At district level it resolved, and resolved well — no Chrome divestiture, no Android breakup, and all three ad tech structural remedies rejected on 2 September. What remains is conduct regulation and an appeal, not dismemberment. Evidence — Phase 1: Mehta remedy terms; Brinkema ruling 2026-09-02.

What the consensus is correctly terrified of

  1. Free cash flow has gone negative for the first time since the 2004 IPO, buybacks are halted, and Alphabet is funding a $200bn-a-year build with debt and the largest equity raise in US corporate history — while still paying a dividend. A genuine change in financial character, in a single year.
  2. ~60% of capex is short-lived compute and the useful-life assumption is unverifiable from outside. Six-year to four-year lives would cut reported earnings by tens of billions with no change in the business — and it sits directly under the two methods carrying 80% of the valuation weight.
  3. FY2027 capex consensus is ~$257bn with no stated peak. Open-ended capital commitment against partly-trusted demand is how capital cycles end badly, and the whole sector is doing it simultaneously.
