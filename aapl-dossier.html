Valuation football field

Every method, in every scenario, lands below the market price. The unanimity matters more than any single figure — this is not one model disagreeing with the tape.

The reverse DCF

Rather than argue about my assumptions, invert the question: what must be true to justify $328?

The price embeds ~5.7% growth in perpetuity. At an 8.8% WACC, terminal growth must reach approximately 5.7% forever to justify $328 — above long-run US nominal GDP of roughly 4%. Stated the other way: holding terminal growth at a defensible 3.5%, the price requires a ~6.9% WACC, an equity risk premium of about 2.7%. The market is pricing Apple closer to a bond than to an equity.

1Pricing-power architecture 12 findings

Who sets the price? Apple does — no regulator, no tariff, no rate case. The vulnerability is not pricing power, it is counterparty concentration.

  • FactHeld prices in China while competitors raised theirs, taking share from 13.9% to 18.1% between Q2 2025 and Q2 2026 — growing ~25% in a market that fell 4.3%. Huawei holds 22.6%.
  • FactGoogle pays an estimated $20bn a year for default search placement — 36% of Safari search ad revenue, close to pure margin.
  • StatuteJudge Mehta's remedy let the payments continue but stripped their protection: no longer exclusive, Google cannot prevent Apple promoting rivals, and revenue share cannot be conditioned on default status beyond 12 months.
  • EstimateThat is roughly 13% of FY2026E operating income at near-100% margin, and it cannot be replaced by pricing. Both sides are appealing — Google on liability (May 2026), the DOJ on remedy (Feb 2026).
  • StatuteFrom 1 Oct 2026 EU commissions become tiered: 26% via Apple IAP, 20% via alternative payment, 15% for small business and second-year subscriptions. The €500m DMA dispute closed 18 Aug 2026.
  • FactNo controlling shareholder and no state ownership — capital returns limited only by cash generation.
2Asset base & physical bottlenecks 9 findings

Apple has taken the opposite side of the AI capex trade. This is the central strategic bet in the name.

  • FactCapex of ~$4bn across H1 FY2026 against $83bn of operating cash flow — under 5%. The differentiating asset, silicon and software integration, does not appear on the balance sheet.
  • FactApple rents frontier AI for ~$1bn a year from Google for Gemini, against tens of billions of hyperscaler capex — roughly 0.25% of what they are committing. The supplier is simultaneously Apple's largest revenue counterparty, antitrust co-defendant and a direct competitor.
  • GuidanceQ4 gross margin guided to 47–48% against 50.1% actual — of which roughly 2pp was non-recurring tariff refunds.
  • EstimateIndian production runs 5–8% above equivalent Chinese cost; tariffs cost ~$900m in a quarter. Apple is absorbing all of it to defend share.
  • GuidanceThe binding constraint in FY2026 was supply, not demand — an unusually good problem that flatters nothing in the valuation.

Methodological consequence: price-to-book and conventional ROIC are excluded from Phase 5 rather than reported as misleadingly high. A company that has repurchased most of its book equity cannot be valued against it.

Why the market is mispricing this

Three non-consensus points. Each states what the market believes, what this analysis concludes, and the specific phase evidence that separates them.

  1. Market believes: Services is the durable annuity that justifies the premium multiple.
    We believe: Services decelerated from +16% to +12% and missed consensus, while hardware — the cyclical line — grew 22%. An annuity multiple is being paid for a mix moving toward cyclicality, not away from it. Evidence — Phase 3: Q2 vs Q3 FY2026 Services growth and the $30.74bn vs $31.22bn consensus miss.
  2. Market believes: Low AI capex is disciplined allocation that will let Apple out-earn the hyperscalers.
    We believe: Apple is renting its most strategically important capability for ~$1bn a year from its largest counterparty, antitrust co-defendant and direct competitor — two years late, with no owned fallback. That is dependency, not discipline. Evidence — Phase 2: the Gemini contract against hyperscaler capex; Siri shipping Sept 2026 after a 2024 demonstration.
  3. Market believes: The antitrust remedy was a win for Apple because the $20bn survived.
    We believe: The payment survived; its protection did not. Non-exclusive, no restriction on promoting rivals, 12-month cap, both sides appealing. ~13% of operating income at near-100% margin moved from a locked contract to an annual renegotiation. Evidence — Phase 1: Mehta remedy terms; Google's May 2026 and the DOJ's Feb 2026 appeals.

What the consensus is correctly terrified of

If you cannot state the bear case better than a bear would, you do not understand the asset.

  1. The valuation genuinely requires perpetual above-GDP growth. This is not a bear talking point — it is what the reverse DCF returns, and it holds across every reasonable input set.
  2. FY2026 is a replacement cycle, and cycles pull demand forward. Q4 guidance already steps down to +9–11% from +16%. Two quarters of +22% iPhone growth against a normal cadence is what a pull-forward looks like before it becomes an air pocket.
  3. ~$20bn of near-costless profit rests on one annually-repriced contract with a company that is both counterparty and competitor. It cannot be replaced by pricing, and neither appeal resolves for years.
