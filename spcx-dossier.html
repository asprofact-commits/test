What the optionality has to be worth

The clearest way to state this investment case is not a multiple. It is the gap between a deliberately generous sum-of-the-parts and the market price — and what that gap implies about assets which are not yet commercially operating.

The reverse valuation

The price treats management's target as an accomplished fact. To justify $1.92tn at a mature 30× EBITDA, the group needs roughly $64bn of EBITDA. At Starlink's 63% margin that implies about $100bn of revenue — precisely management's end-2026 annualised target, against ~$31bn annualised today. The $100bn target is not upside to the price. It is the price.

The five phases

1Pricing-power architecture10 findings

The franchise is not the problem. Pricing power is demonstrable in all three segments — the weakness is governance.

  • FactListed on Nasdaq as SPCX on 12 June 2026 at $135, closing day one at $161. Acquired xAI in February 2026 in an all-stock exchange — SpaceX ~$1tn, xAI ~$250bn, combined ~$1.25tn.
  • FactStarlink reached 12m subscribers, roughly double year on year, with 1.7m net adds in Q2 alone and ~85% of revenue recurring.
  • FactA working constellation of ~10,280 satellites. Amazon Kuiper must buy launches from SpaceX to compete with SpaceX, and was expected to miss its FCC 50%-deployment deadline.
  • EstimateGovernance is the weak point: a related-party all-stock merger ascribed $250bn to xAI on both sides of a transaction controlled by the same person, three months before listing.
  • EstimateKey-man concentration is unusually high, and every normal public-market check on an aggressive valuation is weaker here than for any other company examined under this protocol.
2Asset base & physical bottlenecks9 findings
  • EstimateReuse is the cost curve. A booster flown 20+ times amortises its build cost across 20 missions — the mechanism no competitor can match on price.
  • EstimateBut the constellation depreciates on a ~5-year life. Replacement launch capacity is required simply to stand still, so a large replacement capex is permanent rather than a phase that ends.
  • EstimateSpaceX is its own largest launch customer. That internalises replacement at marginal cost — and means a large share of quoted launch cadence is cost, not revenue.
  • ConsensusConsolidated free cash flow is negative. Starlink's profit is subsidising xAI development; the group does not retain Starlink's ~$8.1bn of 2026 pro forma FCF.
  • GuidanceThe merger's stated rationale — space-based data centres — has no operating facility anywhere. Starship is pre-commercial. Two of the five principal assets do not commercially exist.

The bottleneck is capital, not demand or technology. Starlink adds subscribers faster than the group can simultaneously fund constellation replacement, the Starship ramp and the AI compute build from operating cash flow.

Why the market is mispricing this

  1. Market believes: expensive but justified by optionality — Starship, Mars, space data centres.
    We believe: quantify it. The optionality must carry ~$1.05 trillion — the gap between a generous $875bn sum-of-the-parts and the $1.92tn price. That is more than the entire market capitalisation of all but a handful of companies on earth, ascribed to assets not yet commercially operating. Evidence — Phase 5 SOTP build; Phase 2: Starship pre-commercial, no space data centre in operation.
  2. Market believes: the $100bn ARR target is achievable — Deutsche Bank called it "likely very achievable".
    We believe: it needs ~3× the run rate in two quarters with no disclosed bridge. More importantly, the reverse valuation shows the price already requires it, delivered and sustained at Starlink-like group margins. The target is not upside to the price; it is the price. Evidence — Phase 3: Q2 revenue $7.8bn, ~$31bn annualised, against the $100bn target.
  3. Market believes: the August lockup passed without a crash, so the overhang is behind us.
    We believe: 911.5m unlocked in August; ~12.4bn more by 8 December. All price discovery so far has happened on under 5% of the company. The overhang is almost entirely ahead. Evidence — Phase 4: lockup schedule against the 638.9m IPO float.

What the consensus is correctly terrified of

Stated against my own rating. If you cannot argue the other side better than its advocates, you do not understand the asset.

  1. This analysis may simply be wrong, in the way conventional analysis has been wrong about this company for twenty years. Every conservative valuation of SpaceX since 2012 has been too low. A method that gives zero weight to unproven capability will systematically undervalue a company whose defining characteristic is delivering unproven capability. This is the strongest argument against this rating and it deserves to be stated first.
  2. Starlink is genuinely superb — 12m subscribers doubling year on year, ~85% recurring, an estimated 63% EBITDA margin — and is valued here at 32× EBITDA. If it reaches 40–50m subscribers, the anchor used in this dossier is far too low.
  3. The related-party structure means the usual checks are weak, and the largest single component of the sum-of-the-parts is a mark the controlling shareholder set on both sides of the transaction. No amount of operating excellence offsets that — it is the reason to demand a wider margin of safety here than for a comparable business with dispersed ownership.

Re-entry — an evidence test, not a price

Blended fair value is ~$58; the generous bull sum-of-the-parts is ~$67. Nothing in this work supports buying at $147.95. But the disciplined posture is to hold an evidence test rather than a price target. Any of these would independently justify revisiting: (1) a credible, disclosed bridge to the $100bn run rate; (2) two consecutive quarters of positive consolidated free cash flow; (3) completion of the December lockup at a stable price. Absent those, this is an exceptional business to admire and not to own at this multiple.

The five gaps that matter most

  1. The bridge from $31bn to $100bn. The entire gap between this analysis and the sell-side reduces to it.
  2. Balance sheet — net debt, cash, IPO proceeds. Assumed zero.
  3. Exact share count — ~13bn derived from lockup coverage; a 10% error moves per-share value 10%.
  4. Segment operating income for AI and launch. Two of three SOTP segments are valued on revenue multiples because no earnings figure exists.
  5. The independent basis for the $250bn xAI mark. $350bn of an $875bn SOTP rests on it.
