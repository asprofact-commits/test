# Phase 2 — Productive asset base & physical bottlenecks

**The question:** what actually produces the output, what does each unit cost, and what physically limits expansion?

## 2.1 The asset base and its cost curve

- Inventory the productive assets by type, age, capacity and utilisation. Anchor forward capacity to the company's own published plan or the governing statutory plan, and cite it.
- Build the **unit cost curve**: cost per unit of output by asset type, fully loaded (fuel/input, O&M, capital charge). For utilities this is LCOE by technology; for manufacturers, cost per unit by plant or node; for extractive businesses, breakeven by field.
- **Quantify mix shift.** This is the phase's central arithmetic: when one unit of expensive marginal output is displaced by one unit of cheap baseload output, what is the margin expansion? Show it per unit and in aggregate, with the assumed utilisation. A mix-shift claim without this arithmetic is narrative.
- Retirement and replacement schedule, with the capex required to hold output flat. Capex that merely sustains output is not growth capex — separate the two explicitly.

## 2.2 The bottleneck

Growth plans fail at physical constraints far more often than at demand. Find the binding one.

- Identify the constraint: transmission and interconnection, permitting and rights-of-way, equipment lead times, skilled labour, water, power, land, or licence.
- **Named projects, named delays.** List the specific projects currently delayed, how long, and why. Local opposition, litigation and permitting are the usual causes and they are quantifiable — count the years.
- Where legislation exists to resolve the bottleneck (special permitting acts, eminent-domain reform, subsidy regimes), analyse what it actually changes: who now decides, on what timetable, funded by whom. Then state the execution risk honestly. Enabling legislation is not a completed asset.
- Model the delay: what does a two-year slip in the critical path do to the volumes assumed in Phase 3 and the cash flows in Phase 5?

## 2.3 Capex and its compensation

Tie back to the guardrail. For the announced capital programme:
- Total quantum, phasing, and what share is growth vs maintenance.
- **Is it compensated?** In a regulated business: does it enter the rate base, at what allowed return, and with what lag between spend and recovery? In a competitive one: what incremental return does it earn, and over what asset life?
- Capex spent ahead of recovery is a financing problem before it is a returns problem — size the gap and carry it into Phase 4.

## Required output

- Asset inventory table: type, capacity, utilisation, age.
- Unit cost curve table with sources.
- Mix-shift arithmetic, shown per unit and in aggregate.
- Bottleneck table: project, status, delay, cause, resolution path, execution risk.
- Capex table split growth vs maintenance, compensated vs uncompensated.
