# Sector profiles

The five phases are fixed. What fills them is not. Identify the company's sector first, then apply the profile below — it tells you which mechanics engage, which metrics matter, and which valuation methods carry weight in Phase 5.

If a company spans sectors, run the profile of the segment that drives the majority of EBIT, and handle the rest in the Phase 5 sum-of-the-parts.

## How the phases translate

| Phase | Generic frame | The question that never changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pricing-power architecture | Who sets the price, and what can override that? |
| 2 | Productive asset base & bottlenecks | What produces the output, and what physically limits it? |
| 3 | Demand & monetisation structure | Where does incremental volume come from, and is it margin-accretive? |
| 4 | Capital structure & returns on capital | Can it fund the plan, and does it out-earn its cost of capital? |
| 5 | Valuation, stress tests, decision | What is it worth, and what breaks the thesis? |

## Regulated utility / infrastructure

The mandate's native sector. Everything engages.

- **P1** — Regulator relationship and statutory basis. Tariff-setting mechanics: cost pass-through formulae, fuel-cost adjustment, rate cases, and the exact legal conditions under which a government can suspend or defer an increase (political override is the central risk). Wholesale price caps and how they shelter or expose the regulated entity. State ownership and whether minority shareholder returns can legally be prioritised over industrial policy.
- **P2** — Generation/asset mix, LCOE by technology, capacity plans anchored to the statutory long-range plan, retirement schedules. Network bottlenecks: transmission queues, rights-of-way, permitting regimes, local opposition. Quantify the margin expansion when expensive marginal supply is displaced by cheap baseload.
- **P3** — Large-load interconnections. Are they standard tariff classes or negotiated? Customer capital contributions, wheeling fees, direct PPAs, reliability surcharges.
- **P4** — **Regulated Asset Base (RAB)**: size, allowed return, what share of forward capex enters the rate base. Statutory debt ceilings. Rate and FX sensitivity on fuel imports and hard-currency debt.
- **P5** — EV/RAB is the primary multiple. SOTP splitting regulated network from merchant generation. Peers: NextEra, Duke, Southern, National Grid, Iberdrola, Enel, TEPCO, Kansai, CLP, Taipower.

## Semiconductors & hardware

- **P1** — Pricing is competitive, not regulated: node leadership, customer concentration, export controls and sanctions regimes (these are the statutes that bind). Subsidy regimes as a cost-of-capital input, not revenue.
- **P2** — Fab capacity by node, wafer starts, yield, equipment lead times, capex per incremental wafer. The bottleneck is tools, power and skilled labour.
- **P3** — Design wins, long-term supply agreements, prepayments. Classify demand rigorously — a hyperscaler MOU is not a contract.
- **P4** — Capex intensity vs depreciation, cyclical ROIC through a full cycle, not at the peak.
- **P5** — EV/EBITDA and P/E through-cycle, replacement cost of capacity. Beware valuing peak-cycle earnings on peak multiples.

## Software & internet

- **P1** — Pricing power comes from switching costs, network effects and contract structure. Antitrust and data regulation are the statutory constraints.
- **P2** — Asset base is R&D, sales capacity and compute. The bottleneck is talent and, increasingly, data-centre power.
- **P3** — Net revenue retention, cohort economics, CAC payback, backlog/RPO. Distinguish contracted revenue from pipeline.
- **P4** — Rule of 40, FCF conversion, SBC as a real cost. ROIC on acquired intangibles.
- **P5** — EV/Sales only alongside a path to FCF; DCF with explicit margin maturation. Peers by growth-adjusted multiples.

## Banks & financials

- **P1** — Prudential regulation is the architecture: capital requirements, liquidity rules, supervisory posture, deposit-guarantee regimes.
- **P2** — The "asset base" is the loan book: composition, duration, credit quality, concentration.
- **P3** — Deposit franchise, funding cost, loan growth and its credit quality. Growth in lending at deteriorating spreads is the sector's version of value-destructive volume.
- **P4** — CET1 ratio and headroom, capital return capacity, NIM sensitivity to rate shifts, provisioning adequacy through the cycle.
- **P5** — P/B against normalised ROE is primary; DCF is a dividend-discount or excess-returns model, never an EV/FCF model.

## Energy & commodities

- **P1** — Price is set by the global market, not the company. Licence regimes, royalties, fiscal terms and windfall taxes are the architecture.
- **P2** — Reserves, production decline curves, position on the cost curve, breakeven price.
- **P3** — Offtake contracts, hedging book, spot exposure.
- **P4** — Balance sheet through the trough, not at the peak. Reinvestment ratio vs depletion.
- **P5** — NAV/DCF at strip and at flat long-run prices. EV/EBITDA is cycle-dependent and must be normalised.

## Healthcare & pharma

- **P1** — Approval and reimbursement regimes set the price; patent life sets its duration.
- **P2** — Pipeline by phase and probability of success; manufacturing capacity.
- **P3** — Launch trajectories, payer coverage, patent cliffs as negative demand events.
- **P4** — R&D as capitalised investment; ROIC on acquired assets after impairments.
- **P5** — Risk-adjusted NPV per asset, summed. Multiples secondary.

## Consumer, retail & industrials

- **P1** — Brand strength, channel power, private-label threat, input-cost pass-through ability.
- **P2** — Footprint, plants, distribution, unit economics per store or line.
- **P3** — Like-for-like growth vs space growth — the classic volume-≠-value trap.
- **P4** — Working capital cycle, lease-adjusted leverage, ROIC including leases and goodwill.
- **P5** — EV/EBITDA and P/E vs organic growth and ROIC. DCF with explicit reinvestment.

## Real estate & REITs

- **P1** — Lease law, rent regulation, zoning, REIT distribution requirements.
- **P2** — Portfolio by asset quality, occupancy, WAULT, capex to hold rents flat.
- **P3** — Leasing pipeline, tenant covenant strength, mark-to-market on renewal.
- **P4** — LTV, debt maturity walls, interest cover, cost of debt vs cap rate — the spread is the whole business.
- **P5** — NAV per share against a cap-rate matrix; P/NAV and implied cap rate are primary.

## Choosing valuation methods in Phase 5

Run every method that applies and triangulate. Say which one you weight most and why.

| Method | Engages when |
|---|---|
| DCF (FCFF) | Almost always. Mandatory unless a bank. |
| Asset-base multiple (EV/RAB, EV/IC, P/NAV) | A regulated or asset-heavy base exists |
| P/B vs normalised ROE | Book value is economically meaningful — utilities, banks, insurers |
| SOTP | Separable businesses with different economics or ownership |
| Peer multiples | Always, as a cross-check — never as the primary answer |
| Risk-adjusted NPV | Pipeline-driven businesses |
