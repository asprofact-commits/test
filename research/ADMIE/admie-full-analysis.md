## Phase 1 — Pricing-power architecture

Pricing power here is neither commercial nor competitive — **it is the allowed return.** IPTO cannot raise price, cannot lose share, cannot be disintermediated. What it can suffer is a regulatory reset, and that is the entire risk architecture of the equity.

The growth mechanism is the cleanest in the batch: **~€6bn of investment taking the RAB from ~€3.3bn to ~€7bn by 2029.** In a regulated network, RAB growth converts almost mechanically into earnings growth — no volume risk, no price risk, no counterparty risk.

**But shareholders funded it, and the thesis does not say so.** ADMIE Holding issued **130,864,197 new shares at €4.17** in a non-preemptive book-build completed 18 June 2026, raising ~€530–546m to fund its 51% pro-rata share of IPTO's €1bn capital increase. Against 232m shares previously outstanding, that is a **56% increase**. Non-preemptive means existing holders received no rights.

That makes **two Greek companies in this batch that funded a grid build by materially diluting shareholders in 2026** — PPC by 62% in April, ADMIE Holding by 56% in June. Both descriptions present the plan and omit who paid for it.

And profit moved the wrong way: **FY2025 adjusted net profit fell ~16% to €132.4m from €157.9m**, in a business whose asset base was growing. The cause is not established here, and it is the most important thing this dossier does not know.

## Phase 2 — Asset base: the strongest barrier in the batch

The barrier to entry here is stronger than Constellation's nuclear fleet — because it is a **legal** monopoly rather than merely a physical one. Nobody may build a competing Greek transmission network at any price.

| **RAB 2029 target** | **~€7bn** |
| Capex 2026–2029 | ~€6bn |
| IPTO equity injection | €1bn (ADMIE Holding funded 51%) |

**The finding that qualifies the thesis:**

| Element | Status | IPTO's economic exposure |
|---|---|---|
| **Great Sea Interconnector SPV** | Established Jan 2024 by IPTO | **Meridiam is now MAJORITY shareholder** |
| Crete–Cyprus section | Advanced | Minority, through the SPV |
| Cyprus–Israel section | **Investment request submitted Aug 2026** | Not yet approved |
| Domestic island interconnections | In the RAB plan | Full, through the 51% stake |
| Network reinforcement for renewables | In the RAB plan | Full, through the 51% stake |

**The project the thesis leads with is the one shareholders own least of.** IPTO is the promoter, not the principal owner, and ADMIE Holding reaches it only through a 51% subsidiary.

That de-risking probably cuts in the company's favour. Selling down a technically difficult, geopolitically exposed deep-water Mediterranean cable to a specialist infrastructure fund removes a large execution risk from an equity whose entire appeal is safety. **The value sits in the unglamorous domestic build.**

One shared exposure: IPTO buys into the same **128-week transformer lead times** that make Siemens Energy valuable. For a regulated operator the sting is milder — cost overruns are typically added to the RAB — but *schedule* slippage delays the RAB growth the whole case rests on.

A temporary dip in cover during a doubling of the asset base is not a broken business. But it is unresolved today, and the thesis calls this a yield investment.

### Why BUY (small) against a model HOLD

The model returns +8%, which reads as HOLD. I am stating **BUY, small**, for a structural rather than arithmetic reason: this is the only business in the batch with **no volume, price or counterparty risk**; its **bear case is only ~8% below the current price**; and it is the one company here whose dilution bought a *regulated* return rather than a merchant one. It is sized **small** because the dividend cover on the enlarged share count is genuinely unresolved and look-through net debt is derived rather than sourced.

### Three non-consensus points

1. **The defensive half is right; the yield half is unresolved.** ~€32m of look-through FY2025 receipts is ~€0.09/share against €0.18–0.24 expectations costing ~€65m. Data services disagree by 2×, suggesting at least one hasn't been updated for the June issue.
2. **The flagship interconnector is Meridiam-majority.** IPTO is promoter, not principal owner, reached through a 51% subsidiary — and the Cyprus–Israel section was only submitted for approval in August 2026. The value is in the domestic RAB.
3. **Two Greek dilutions, not one proposition.** PPC +62% to build merchant generation while targeting a rising dividend; ADMIE Holding +56% at a modest discount to recapitalise a legal monopoly. Diluting to fund a regulated return is a different act.

### What could go wrong

1. **A WACC reset.** The one risk that can impair a regulated network, entirely outside the company's control. A 50bp cut removes most of the upside here.
2. **A dividend rebasing.** For a stock owned almost entirely for yield, a cut hits both the income and the multiple at once — the strongest argument against the rating, and why the position is small.
3. **The upside is small relative to the inputs.** A 25% error in derived look-through net debt moves fair value by ~€0.49 — more than the entire 8% gap.

### Trigger-to-sell
- Any cut to the allowed WACC at a regulatory reset
- DPS declared below €0.12 on the enlarged share count
- RAB progression falling materially behind the €7bn 2029 trajectory
- IPTO net debt / RAB above 55%
- A further capital increase at either level
- Adjusted net profit falling for a second consecutive year
- Any move to bring interconnector assets outside the remunerated perimeter

### Re-entry test
Below **~€4.10** the shares sit at or under 1.0× look-through RAB with a base-case IRR above 20% — a full position. At €4.49, small. The evidentiary test is a single disclosure: **the first dividend declared on the enlarged share count.** A payout at or above €0.18 covered by rising look-through receipts confirms the thesis and justifies sizing up; a rebasing would not break the RAB story but would remove the reason most holders own it.

### Largest gaps
1. **Dividend policy on the enlarged share count** — the most important unknown here.
2. **IPTO net debt and gearing** — derived; a 25% error exceeds the entire upside.
3. **The allowed WACC and reset timetable** — the entire return architecture.
4. **The cause of the 16% FY2025 profit decline.**
5. **IPTO's residual stake in the Great Sea Interconnector.**
