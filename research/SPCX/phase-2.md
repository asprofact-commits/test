# SpaceX — Phase 2: Asset base & physical bottlenecks

**Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SpaceX)** · NASDAQ: SPCX · $147.95 · ~$1.92tn · 2026-09-05

> **Evidence note.** No primary filing was retrievable — every external domain is blocked by this environment's egress proxy, including `sec.gov`. Findings rest on search-result summaries. This matters more here than in any other dossier: **SpaceX has been public for under three months and has reported two quarters.**

## An unmatched asset base — that depreciates

**~10,280 satellites** in a working constellation, plus a reusable fleet flying boosters **more than 20 times each**. Reuse *is* the cost curve: a booster flown 20 times amortises its build cost across 20 missions, which is why no competitor can match the price.

But Starlink satellites have roughly a **five-year design life**. The constellation needs continuous replacement launch capacity *simply to stand still*. That is the elegance of the model — SpaceX is its own largest launch customer, so replacement is internalised at marginal cost — and also its trap: a large share of quoted launch cadence is **cost, not revenue**.

## Where the cash actually goes

| Flow | Direction | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Starlink operating income | **Generates** | $1.19bn in Q1 2026 on $3.26bn revenue |
| Constellation replacement | Consumes | ~5-year life on ~10,280 satellites |
| Starship development | Consumes | Pre-commercial ramp |
| xAI compute build | Consumes | "Soaring AI costs" cited in the first post-IPO report |
| **Consolidated free cash flow** | **Negative** | Starlink profit subsidising xAI development |

**The only segment disclosed as profitable is funding three that are not.**

## Two of the five principal assets do not commercially exist

Starship is pre-commercial and carries the entire bull case for future launch economics. **Space-based data centres** — the stated rationale for a $250bn acquisition — have no operating facility anywhere.

## The bottleneck is capital

Not demand, not technology. Starlink adds subscribers faster than the group can simultaneously fund constellation replacement, the Starship ramp and the AI compute build from operating cash flow.

## What this phase establishes
1. The operating asset base is genuinely unmatched and the cost curve is real.
2. It depreciates on ~5 years, so replacement capex is permanent, not a phase that ends.
3. The binding constraint is capital.
4. Consolidated FCF is negative; one profitable segment funds three that are not.
5. Two of five principal assets are prospective rather than operating.

The operating assets are exceptional; **the prospective assets carry the valuation.** Phase 5 must be explicit about how much of the price rests on what is operating and how much on what is not.
