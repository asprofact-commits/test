# SpaceX — Phase 1: Pricing-power architecture

**Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SpaceX)** · NASDAQ: SPCX · $147.95 · ~$1.92tn · 2026-09-05

> **Evidence note.** No primary filing was retrievable — every external domain is blocked by this environment's egress proxy, including `sec.gov`. Findings rest on search-result summaries. ## The company is no longer private

SpaceX listed on Nasdaq as **SPCX on 12 June 2026** at $135, closing day one at $161 (+19%). It trades at **$147.95**.

In **February 2026** it acquired **xAI** in an all-stock exchange — SpaceX at ~$1tn, xAI at ~$250bn, combined ~$1.25tn. xAI holders received 0.1433 SpaceX shares each, with a cash alternative at $75.46.

## Pricing power is real in all three segments

| Segment | Who sets price | Evidence | Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starlink** | SpaceX list price | **12m subscribers**, doubled YoY; 1.7m net adds in Q2; ~85% recurring | Capacity, spectrum, Kuiper |
| **Launch** | Near-monopoly on reusable capacity | 150+ flights targeted 2026; boosters reused **20+ times** | FAA licensing |
| **xAI / AI** | Contract and usage | Q2 revenue **$2.6bn, +247%** | Compute cost, frontier competition |

The moat in connectivity is wide and widening: **~10,280 satellites** in orbit, and Amazon Kuiper must buy launches **from SpaceX** to compete with SpaceX — while expected to miss its FCC 50%-deployment deadline of July 2026.

Unusually for this protocol, **the franchise is not the problem.**

## The problem is governance

| Issue | Character |
|---|---|
| **Related-party merger** | SpaceX acquired xAI — both Musk-controlled, all-stock, self-valued. The $250bn ascribed to xAI was not set by an arm's-length market. |
| **Key-man concentration** | One individual sets roadmap, capital allocation and narrative across launch, connectivity and AI. No disclosed succession. |
| **Listing recency** | Two quarters of public reporting. No track record of guidance versus delivery. |
| **Lockup overhang** | ~13bn shares against **639m floated**. Full expiry 8 December 2026. |

## What this phase establishes
1. Pricing power is demonstrable in all three segments.
2. The connectivity moat is wide and widening — Kuiper funds its own competitor.
3. The weakness is governance, not competition.
4. Every normal public-market check on an aggressive valuation is weaker here than in any other company examined under this protocol.
5. ~95% of the share base was locked at listing.

## Could not establish
- **The S-1 and first 10-Q read in full** — close to disqualifying for a committee decision on a three-month-old listing
- Whether an independent committee and fairness opinion supported the $250bn xAI mark
- Starlink ARPU and churn

## What it changes
Inverts the usual shape of this protocol. The risk is not that the franchise fails — it is that an exceptional franchise has been priced by a market with unusually little information and unusually weak checks.

# SpaceX — Phase 2: Asset base & physical bottlenecks

**Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SpaceX)** · NASDAQ: SPCX · $147.95 · ~$1.92tn · 2026-09-05

> **Evidence note.** No primary filing was retrievable — every external domain is blocked by this environment's egress proxy, including `sec.gov`. Findings rest on search-result summaries. This matters more here than in any other dossier: **SpaceX has been public for under three months and has reported two quarters.**

## An unmatched asset base — that depreciates

**~10,280 satellites** in a working constellation, plus a reusable fleet flying boosters **more than 20 times each**. Reuse *is* the cost curve: a booster flown 20 times amortises its build cost across 20 missions, which is why no competitor can match the price.

But Starlink satellites have roughly a **five-year design life**. The constellation needs continuous replacement launch capacity *simply to stand still*. That is the elegance of the model — SpaceX is its own largest launch customer, so replacement is internalised at marginal cost — and also its trap: a large share of quoted launch cadence is **cost, not revenue**.

## Where the cash actually goes

| Flow | Direction | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Starlink operating income | **Generates** | $1.19bn in Q1 2026 on $3.26bn revenue |
| Constellation replacement | Consumes | ~5-year life on ~10,280 satellites |
| Starship development | Consumes | Pre-commercial ramp |
| xAI compute build | Consumes | "Soaring AI costs" cited in the first post-IPO report |
| **Consolidated free cash flow** | **Negative** | Starlink profit subsidising xAI development |

**The only segment disclosed as profitable is funding three that are not.**

## Two of the five principal assets do not commercially exist

Starship is pre-commercial and carries the entire bull case for future launch economics. **Space-based data centres** — the stated rationale for a $250bn acquisition — have no operating facility anywhere.

## The bottleneck is capital

Not demand, not technology. Starlink adds subscribers faster than the group can simultaneously fund constellation replacement, the Starship ramp and the AI compute build from operating cash flow.

## What this phase establishes
1. The operating asset base is genuinely unmatched and the cost curve is real.
2. It depreciates on ~5 years, so replacement capex is permanent, not a phase that ends.
3. The binding constraint is capital.
4. Consolidated FCF is negative; one profitable segment funds three that are not.
5. Two of five principal assets are prospective rather than operating.

## Could not establish
- Capex guidance and its split by segment
- Depreciation policy and assumed satellite life
- Third-party launch revenue versus internal Starlink launches
- Any operating evidence for space-based data centres

## What it changes
The operating assets are exceptional; **the prospective assets carry the valuation.** Phase 5 must be explicit about how much of the price rests on what is operating and how much on what is not.

# SpaceX — Phase 3: Demand & monetisation structure

**Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SpaceX)** · NASDAQ: SPCX · $147.95 · ~$1.92tn · 2026-09-05

This matters more here than in any other dossier: **SpaceX has been public for under three months and has reported two quarters.**

## The first public quarter was strong

| Measure | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|
| Revenue | **$7.8bn**, +92% YoY (beat $6.81bn consensus) |
| Adjusted EBITDA | **$3.5bn**, +191% |
| **Net income** | **−$541m** |
| Connectivity | $4.3bn, **1.7m net subscriber adds** |
| AI segment | $2.6bn, **+247%** |

Starlink reached **12m subscribers** by mid-2026 (roughly double YoY), projected 16.8m by year end. In FY2025 it produced $11.39bn of revenue — 61% of company sales — and was the **only profitable business** at $4.42bn of segment income. For 2026: ~$20bn revenue, ~$14bn EBITDA, ~63% margin, **~85% recurring**.

Growth is margin-accretive at the EBITDA line (+191% on +92% revenue) — **but not at the net line**, where the company still lost $541m.

## The decisive number

Management projects a **$100bn annualised revenue run rate by end-2026**. Q2 revenue of $7.8bn annualises to **~$31bn**.

That implies roughly **tripling the run rate in two quarters**, and no bridge has been disclosed. Deutsche Bank called it "likely very achievable."

## Demand classified

| Source | Evidence | Class | Base-case weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starlink subscriber base | 12m subs, ~85% recurring | `[Existing Contracted]` | Full |
| Government / defence launch | Incumbent manifest | `[Existing Contracted]` | Full |
| AI segment revenue | $2.6bn, +247% | `[Approved / Committed]` | Partial — very short history |
| Starship commercial ramp | Pre-commercial | `[Unfunded Planned]` | Minimal |
| Space-based data centres | No operating facility | `[Speculative]` | **Zero** |
| **$100bn ARR by end-2026** | No disclosed bridge | **`[Speculative]`** | **Zero until bridged** |

**Two of the six rows carry the valuation, and both are speculative under this protocol's own test.**

Unlike Alphabet — which could point to **$514bn of contracted backlog** to justify its capital programme — SpaceX has disclosed no equivalent order book.

## What this phase establishes
1. Operating momentum is genuine and rapid.
2. Starlink is a superb business and the only profitable segment.
3. The company still lost $541m at the net line; adjusted EBITDA excludes the costs that matter.
4. The $100bn target is speculative until bridged — and earns zero base-case weight.
5. No backlog disclosure, so the capital programme cannot be classed as demand-backed.

## Could not establish
- **The bridge from $31bn to $100bn** — the single most valuable disclosure the company could make
- Starlink ARPU and churn
- Whether AI segment revenue is third-party or intra-group
- Any order backlog

## What it changes
Draws the line between what is operating and what is claimed. Starlink at 12m subscribers is real, profitable and compounding. The rest is not yet evidenced.

# Investment Committee Memo

**Rating: `AVOID`** — a price call on an exceptional company, stated with **lower confidence** than the Apple or Alphabet ratings.

Published targets span **$160 (Argus) to $300 (Morgan Stanley)** — an 88% spread. **This analysis sits ~64% below the lowest of them.** That divergence is methodological, not factual: the sell-side capitalises Starship economics, space-based data centres and a multi-year ramp, which this protocol classifies as speculative demand and assigns **zero base-case weight**.

## Why the market is mispricing this — 3 non-consensus points

1. **Market believes:** expensive but justified by optionality — Starship, Mars, space data centres.
   **We believe:** quantify it. The optionality must carry **~$1.05 trillion** — the gap between a generous $875bn sum-of-the-parts and the $1.92tn price. That is more than the entire market capitalisation of all but a handful of companies on earth, ascribed to assets **not yet commercially operating**.
   *Evidence — Phase 5 SOTP build; Phase 2: Starship pre-commercial, no space data centre in operation.*

2. **Market believes:** the $100bn ARR target is achievable — Deutsche Bank called it "likely very achievable".
   **We believe:** it requires ~3× the run rate in two quarters with no disclosed bridge. More importantly, **the reverse valuation shows the price already requires it**, delivered and sustained at Starlink-like group margins. The target is not upside to the price; it *is* the price.
   *Evidence — Phase 3: Q2 revenue $7.8bn, ~$31bn annualised, vs the $100bn target.*

3. **Market believes:** the August lockup passed without a crash, so the overhang is behind us.
   **We believe:** 911.5m unlocked in August; **~12.4bn more by 8 December**. All price discovery so far has happened on **under 5%** of the company. The overhang is almost entirely ahead.
   *Evidence — Phase 4: lockup schedule against the 638.9m IPO float.*

## What the consensus is correctly terrified of — stated against my own rating

1. **This analysis may simply be wrong, in the way conventional analysis has been wrong about this company for twenty years.** Every conservative valuation of SpaceX since 2012 has been too low. A method giving zero weight to unproven capability will systematically undervalue a company whose defining characteristic is delivering unproven capability. *This is the strongest argument against my own rating and it deserves to be stated first.*
2. **Starlink is genuinely superb** — 12m subscribers doubling YoY, ~85% recurring, ~63% EBITDA margin — and is valued here at 32× EBITDA. If it reaches 40–50m subscribers, the anchor used here is far too low.
3. **The related-party structure means the usual checks are weak**, and the largest single SOTP component is a mark the controlling shareholder set on both sides of the transaction. No amount of operating excellence offsets that; it is the reason to demand a wider margin of safety here than for a comparable business with dispersed ownership.

## Trigger-to-sell

- Q3 2026 revenue **below $9bn**, implying no credible path to $100bn ARR
- Starlink net adds **below 1.2m** in any quarter
- Consolidated FCF still negative in FY2027 with **no guided crossover**
- **Any equity issuance at or below** the prevailing price to fund the AI build
- Starship failing a **monthly cadence by end-2027**
- Disclosure that xAI segment revenue is **substantially intra-group**

## Re-entry — an evidence test, not a price

Blended fair value ~$58; the generous bull SOTP ~$67. Nothing here supports buying at $147.95. But the disciplined posture is to hold an **evidence test** rather than a price target. Any of these would independently justify revisiting:

1. A credible, **disclosed bridge** to the $100bn run rate
2. **Two consecutive quarters** of positive consolidated free cash flow
3. Completion of the **December lockup at a stable price**

Absent those, this is an exceptional business to admire and not to own at this multiple.

## Appendix — how this analysis was produced

Each phase ran as a separate stage of the `/invest` protocol, writing structured JSON (`research/SPCX/phase-1…5.json`) alongside these memos. The valuation arithmetic is **not** written by hand: the JSON supplies inputs — cash flows, discount rates, segment multiples, scenario probabilities and method weights — and `research.html` computes the DCF, the sum-of-the-parts, the multiple valuations, the probability weighting and the IRRs. Every figure in Phase 5 is reproducible by loading those files into the workspace and can be flexed there.

**Method weights** for this company were set explicitly to sum-of-the-parts 65%, EV/EBITDA 25%, DCF 10%, overriding the protocol defaults. The reason is in Phase 5: with free cash flow negative across the explicit forecast period, a DCF measures the terminal assumption almost exclusively, and its bear case returns under $1 per share — a demonstration that the instrument fails on this company, not a forecast.

**Taxonomy.** Every finding carries one of five labels — `[FACT]`, `[REGULATORY STATUTE]`, `[MANAGEMENT GUIDANCE]`, `[CONSENSUS]`, `[ANALYST ESTIMATE]` — plus a source and an as-of date. A figure without all three is not usable by an investment committee. Analyst estimates show their derivation.

**Not investment advice.** This applies a fixed protocol to figures gathered from secondary sources and shows its whole reasoning so it can be argued with. Labels record what a source claimed, not independent verification. Check primary sources before acting on any of it.
