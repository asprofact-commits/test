## Phase 1 — Pricing-power architecture

The structure is as described: a vertically integrated incumbent spanning regulated distribution, generation and retail supply. But **only part of the earnings base is genuinely regulated, and it is not wholly owned.** HEDNO is the regulated asset, and PPC sold 49% to Macquarie — so a substantial share of the most defensive cash flow in the group accrues to minorities. Everything else — generation and supply across five countries — competes on price in liberalised markets.

The transformation is genuinely well advanced: **7.3 GW of renewables at H1 2026, up 1 GW YoY and now 58% of installed capacity**, with the lignite phase-out completing in 2026 and 40% of island oil-fired generation to be decommissioned. Operating delivery is good — H1 adjusted EBITDA €1.24bn, and the FY2025 €2.0bn target was hit.

**What fails is "low-volatility yield investment."** A company that raised €4.5bn of equity in April 2026 to fund a €24.2bn build is not harvesting a rate base. It is building one, and shareholders are paying for it.

Roughly **45% of the 2030 target sits outside Greece**, in markets where PPC is a challenger rather than an incumbent. Execution risk is concentrated precisely where the company has least history.

| Constraint | Nature | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment supply | ~128-week transformer lead times, prices +77% since 2019 | **PPC is the buyer** — the wrong side of this batch's bottleneck |
| Greek solar capture prices | Curtailment, negative midday pricing | Each incremental MW may earn less than the last |
| Spending rate | €2.8bn annualised vs ~€4.8bn/yr required | Must rise materially, into an inflating supply chain |
| New-market execution | ~45% of 2030 capacity outside Greece | Challenger position |
| Regulated asset ownership | 51% of HEDNO | The defensive asset is minority-shared |

There is a neat irony across this batch: **PPC buys the transformers and turbines whose scarcity makes Siemens Energy and GE Vernova valuable.** A €24.2bn programme executed into that market carries cost and schedule risk a plan drawn in 2026 may not fully price. The plan's inclusion of storage alongside renewables is the correct response to curtailment — and an implicit admission of the problem.

## Phase 4 — Capital structure: the 62% nobody mentions

**PPC raised €4.5bn in April 2026, issuing 228m new shares against 369m existing — a 62% increase in the share count.** The thesis describes the plan the money funds and not the dilution that funded it. That is the largest single omission in any of the seven descriptions in this batch, because it changes every per-share figure by a factor of 1.62.

The funding structure itself is conservative and well disclosed: **54% from operations, 31% from new debt, 15% from the equity raise**, sized to keep net debt/EBITDA well below the 3.5× ceiling. *Balance-sheet discipline is not the problem.*

**The multiples diverge sharply, and the divergence is the finding:**

| | At €22.90 |
|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA | **~9.2×** — looks inexpensive |
| P/E on adjusted earnings | **~19.5×** — looks expensive |
| Price / book | ~1.24× |
| **Return on equity** | **~6.4%** — below any reasonable cost of equity |

Both multiples are true. The gap is depreciation, interest and Macquarie's 49% of HEDNO. On book value the picture is clearest: a normalised post-build ROE of ~8.8% against an ~8.5% cost of equity justifies only about **1.05× book** — the honest ceiling until the assets earn.

**The dividend against the raise:**

| Flow | Amount | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| April 2026 capital increase | €4.5bn | **From** shareholders |
| Dividends 2026–30 (€0.80 → €1.40) | ~€3.3bn | **To** shareholders |
| Net equity injection | ~€1.2bn | From shareholders |
| Debt increase funding the plan | ~€7.5bn | 31% of €24.2bn |

Raising €4.5bn and returning €3.3bn of it over the same period is defensible as dividend-policy signalling. It is not obviously the most efficient way to fund a build, and it deserves to be said.

**The most useful tension here:** fair value sits ~12% *below* the price while the expected three-year IRR is *positive* at ~6.4%. **The dividend does that work.** In the base case an investor earns the yield and waits for the build, and earns very little from a re-rating.

**Reverse valuation.** At €22.90 the price implies ~9.2× EBITDA and ~1.24× book against a current ROE of 6.4%. To justify 1.24× book, normalised ROE must reach ~9.8% — about **340bp above where it stands**. The price is a specific bet that €24.2bn of new assets earn well above the current fleet's return.

### Three non-consensus points

1. **A "defensive yield investment" does not require a €4.5bn equity call.** 228m new shares against 369m existing — 62% dilution — to fund the build. Any per-share figure drawn from pre-raise data is overstated by 1.62×.
2. **The Kozani campus has no counterparty.** PPC funds ~a third of a €3.5bn Phase I; four hyperscalers are in talks; none is signed. Constellation signed 920 MW at 18.5 years. PPC has a shortlist.
3. **Cheap on enterprise value, expensive on earnings — both true.** ~9.2× EBITDA vs ~19.5× earnings. The gap is depreciation, interest and Macquarie's 49% of the only genuinely defensive asset in the group.

### What the bulls are correctly confident about

1. **The demand is genuinely inevitable.** SEE grids must be expanded and interconnected, the lignite exit is policy-backed rather than contested, and PPC is the incumbent positioned to do it. Betting against a policy-backed, physically necessary build-out has a poor base rate.
2. **A signed Kozani tenant would change the case quickly** — converting 300 MW to committed, de-risking part of the Greek renewable build against curtailment, and justifying a higher multiple. A knowable event with real probability, not a hope.
3. **The dividend is substantial, growing and covered.** €0.80 → €1.40 by 2030, ~24% compound from €0.40 in 2024. It produces a positive expected return even from a fair value below the price — and it is what makes HOLD rather than AVOID the honest call.

### Trigger-to-sell
- Kozani partner process concluding without agreement, or lapsing past end-2026
- FY2026 adjusted EBITDA below €2.3bn
- Any reduction to the €0.80 dividend or the €1.40 2030 target
- Net debt/EBITDA above 3.0×, approaching the 3.5× threshold
- **A further equity raise of any size** — a second dilution would confirm the plan is underfunded
- Plan capex guided materially above €24.2bn on equipment cost inflation
- Greek capture prices falling faster than the storage build can offset

### Re-entry test
Below **~€18** the shares sit under all three valuation methods with a 4.4% yield and a base-case IRR above 14% — a full position. At €22.90 the appropriate holding is **income-sized, not growth-sized**. The evidentiary test is a single event rather than a price: **a signed Kozani anchor tenant with a disclosed PPA term.** That one announcement would move 300 MW from `[Unfunded Planned]` to committed and would legitimately re-rate the equity.

### Largest gaps
1. **Whether a Kozani anchor tenant has been signed since May 2026** — the most valuable news this company could produce, and unverifiable from here.
2. **Reported net debt and minority interest** — both derived; together comparable in size to the 12% gap.
3. **Regulated vs competitive EBITDA split** — carries 25% of the valuation weight and determines the right multiple for the whole.
4. **Achieved capture prices on the Greek renewable fleet.**
