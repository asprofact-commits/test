### Six findings that only appear when you look across all seven

#### 1. The advance-payment tell — found twice, independently

The single most valuable finding in the batch, and it appeared in two companies with the same mechanism at different intensities.

| | Guided FCF | Against | Ratio | Book-to-bill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GE Vernova** | $11.5–12.5bn | ~$6bn adj EBITDA | **~2.0×** | Orders +88% |
| **Siemens Energy** | ~€8bn pre-tax | ~€5.3bn profit before SI | **~1.5×** | **1.57×** |

Both companies are generating free cash flow far above earnings because **customers are prepaying against order books growing faster than they convert to revenue.** Management at Siemens Energy names the cause explicitly; GE Vernova raised FCF guidance ~$5bn while leaving the EBITDA margin guidance untouched at 12–14% — *that is the tell.*

Neither is an accounting irregularity. Both stop when book-to-bill reaches 1.0×. **Capitalising the guided figure rather than the underlying one is worth roughly 40% of fair value in each case**, and it accounts for most of the gap between this analysis and the sell-side on both names.

#### 2. The scarcity premium is being competed away — by the people it enriches

| Company | Expansion | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Siemens Energy | ~€2bn to expand transformer + GIS capacity **~50% by 2030** | First plants produce 2027 |
| Hitachi Energy | >$1bn to North American transformer capacity | Largest US LPT plant by 2028 |
| GE Vernova | Turbine output 20 GW → **30 GW by 2030** | Queue falls 5.8 yrs → **<4 yrs** |

Every beneficiary of the shortage is simultaneously funding its end. **A transformer plant costs a few hundred million and takes about three years** — which dates the risk precisely. The binding constraint is more likely skilled labour and grain-oriented electrical steel than capital.

This is why GE Vernova at ~40× EBITDA is the batch's one AVOID: it is priced for a scarcity its own capital plan removes.

#### 3. "Hyperscaler PPAs" — four companies, four very different realities

| Company | What was actually signed | Class |
|---|---|---|
| **Vistra** | AWS up to 1,200 MW + Meta ~2.6 GW, **20-year terms** — but 8.6% of the fleet | `[Existing Contracted]` |
| **Constellation** | 920 MW at 18.5-yr avg — **counterparty was Walmart**, ~70% still merchant | `[Existing Contracted]` |

The demand-classification test earned its keep here. **Every description in this set overstated the contracted position** — Vistra by conflating a superb contract with a de-risked fleet, Constellation by ~3× on counterparty scope, PPC by presenting a shortlist as a project.

#### 5. Buyers and sellers of the same bottleneck

The cleanest way to sort this batch:

|---|---|

The sellers are being valued on a shortage they are ending. The buyers face cost and schedule risk from it — **though for the regulated buyers (PPC, ADMIE) cost overruns are largely recoverable through the RAB; only *schedule* slippage genuinely hurts.**

And it cuts back the other way for Vistra: if the turbine queue blocks new ERCOT supply for five years, existing dispatchable capacity is scarcer than the forward curve implies. **This batch supplies its own strongest bull case.**

| GE Vernova | $599 | $1,236 avg (30 buys, **0 sells**) | −52% |
| Vistra | $132 | $217 avg (Strong Buy) | −39% |

**Seven for seven below the covering universe is a pattern, not seven coincidences.** Two honest readings:

- **It is a real edge.** The protocol makes two adjustments consensus appears not to: it capitalises *underlying* rather than advance-inflated free cash flow, and it gives zero base-case weight to speculative demand. Both are deliberate and defensible, and both cut the same direction in a sector where order books are booming.
- **It is a systematic bias.** Assigning zero weight to `[Speculative Demand]` will always undervalue companies whose speculative demand materialises — which, in a genuine structural boom, is most of them. A protocol built to resist narrative will systematically underprice a real one.

I lean to the first but cannot rule out the second, and the base rate favours the analysts: when an entire covering universe sits on the other side, they are usually right. **The protocol's value here is less the price targets than the mechanism findings** — the advance-payment tell, the depleting backlog clock, the contracted-versus-merchant splits. Those stand independently of whether the fair values are calibrated correctly.

### The thesis, tested

Your description called GEV "the premier global infrastructure equity, benefiting from a record $176 billion backlog." **Every operating claim in that sentence is confirmed.** The valuation implication is not.

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| $176bn backlog | ✅ **Confirmed** |
| Data centres and utilities securing turbines | ✅ Confirmed — Power orders **+134%** |
| Grid electrification demand | ✅ Confirmed — Electrification guided $14.5–15.0bn at 18–20% margin |
| "Premier" franchise | ✅ Defensible |
| Implied: therefore buy | ❌ **Not tested by those facts** |

### Phase 1 — Pricing-power architecture

Genuine oligopoly: one of three credible heavy-duty gas turbine suppliers globally. Q2 2026 orders **$24.2bn (+88% organic)**, Power orders **+134%**, gas turbine backlog **116 GW** targeting 125 GW by year-end.

**The central tension.** Management guides 20 GW annual output now → 24 GW in 2028 → **30 GW in 2030**. That 50% capacity expansion *is the mechanism that ends the shortage premium the equity is priced on* — and all three suppliers are expanding simultaneously.

Segment margins tell the real story: Power 17–19%, Electrification 18–20%, but **consolidated only 12–14%**. Wind is the reason — orders **−40%** to $1.2bn. The company carries a structurally loss-making business alongside two excellent ones.

### Phase 2 — Asset base & the backlog clock

The backlog is a **stock that depletes**, not an annuity:

| | Value | Queue length |
|---|---|---|
| Gas turbine backlog | 116 GW | — |
| Current output | 20 GW | **5.8 years** |
| 2028 target | 24 GW | 4.8 years |
| 2030 target | 30 GW | **3.9 years** |

A 5.8-year queue supports scarcity pricing. A sub-4-year queue with three suppliers expanding does not.

Electrification faces a **tighter and longer-lived** bottleneck than turbines — transformers and switchgear have less new entry — which arguably makes it the better business inside the group.

### Phase 3 — Demand & the guidance tell

Q2 2026: revenue **$11.1bn** (+22%, +12% organic), net income **$0.6bn** (5.8% margin), adj EBITDA **$1.2bn** (11.3%).

**The finding that drives this dossier.** FY2026 guidance:

| Measure | Guidance | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $45.5–46.5bn | Raised |
| Adj EBITDA margin | 12–14% | **Unchanged** |
| **Free cash flow** | **$11.5–12.5bn** | **Raised from $6.5–7.5bn** |

That is **free cash flow at roughly 2× adjusted EBITDA** (~$12bn vs ~$6bn). In a capital-equipment business that only happens through **large customer advance payments** on an order book growing 88%.

Those are deposits on equipment not yet built — a **liability, not earnings** — and they reverse as backlog converts or if order growth slows. Q2 FCF of $5.1bn exceeded all of 2025.

**Management raised cash flow guidance by ~$5bn while leaving margin guidance unchanged. That is the tell.**

### Phase 4 — What the market is capitalising

| Basis | Multiple |
|---|---|
| EV / FY2026E adj EBITDA | **~40×** — the honest operating multiple |
| Market cap / *guided* FCF | ~21× — flattered by advances |
| Market cap / *estimated underlying* FCF | **~67×** — the comparable figure |
| Reported P/E (market data) | 26.96×–31.44× — **cannot be reconciled** with $0.6bn quarterly net income |

Estimated underlying FCF is ~$3.5–4.0bn, roughly **a third** of the guided figure. The spread between 21× and 67× depending on which cash flow you use is the single most important thing to understand here.

ROIC cannot be assessed — two years since separation, mid-cycle, mid-expansion.

### Phase 5 — Valuation & IC memo

| Method | Value | vs $943 | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA @ 28× | $669 | −29% | 35% |
| Sum of the parts | $642 | −32% | 25% |
| DCF *(on underlying FCF)* | $512 | −46% | 40% |
| **Blended** | **$599** | **−36%** | 100% |

**Reverse valuation.** To justify $251bn at a generous 20× EBITDA, the business needs **~$12.5bn of EBITDA** — roughly double FY2026 guidance — implying **~$90bn of revenue**, also roughly double. That is achievable by 2030 *if scarcity pricing survives the capacity build*. The price is a specific bet on exactly the tension Phase 1 identified.

#### Three non-consensus points

1. **The FCF guidance shows the balance sheet, not earnings power.** Raised ~$5bn while EBITDA margin guidance was untouched.
2. **The backlog is a depleting stock.** The company is deliberately halving the scarcity that justifies its pricing.
3. **"Premier franchise" and "premier price" are different propositions**, and the thesis conflates them. 40× EBITDA on 12–14% consolidated margins with a loss-making Wind segment.

#### What the consensus is correctly terrified of

1. **30 analysts say buy, none say sell, and the lowest target is the current price.** When the entire covering universe is on the other side, the base rate favours them.
2. **The demand is physical and contracted** — construction projects with financing, not narrative. The bear case rests on *pricing* normalising, not demand disappointing.
3. **Electrification may be structurally better than credited** — transformer shortages could outlast the turbine cycle by years.

#### Trigger-to-sell
- FCF below adjusted EBITDA for two consecutive quarters — confirms the advance reversal
- Order intake growth below 10% YoY for two consecutive quarters
- Gas turbine backlog below 110 GW without a price increase
- Consolidated adj EBITDA margin failing to exceed 14% in FY2027
- A competitor announcing turbine capacity beyond 25 GW annually

#### Largest gaps
1. **Change in contract liabilities** — would settle whether the DCF should use $12bn or $4bn. Nothing else matters as much.
2. Competitor capacity expansion — decides whether the shortage ends 2028 or 2032
3. Backlog margin profile and cancellation terms
4. Sourced net cash and WACC components

### Phase 1 — Pricing-power architecture

Pricing power here is contractual and physical at once. The product cannot be built quickly, cannot be sited freely, and cannot be replicated by renewables-plus-storage at the reliability level a hyperscaler contracts for. That is why a premium exists at all.

But the equity owns the whole fleet, not just the contracted part:

| Output | Basis | Duration | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~30% of clean baseload | Long-term PPAs, investment-grade counterparties | **18.5 years avg** | Genuinely premium, genuinely locked |
| **~70% of clean baseload** | **Merchant / wholesale** | Spot to short | **Exposed to power prices and PJM capacity auctions** |
| Calpine gas fleet | Dispatchable, merchant | Short | Cyclical; hedges the nuclear position |
| Retail supply | Customer contracts | Short–medium | Margin-driven, competitive |

**Regulatory dependence is unusually high and cuts both ways.** Licence extensions, PJM capacity auction design, state clean-energy credits and federal PTCs all move realised price, and none are under company control. Higher PJM capacity prices were a *cited driver of the Q2 beat* — an administrative auction outcome, not a commercial win.

### Phase 2 — Asset base & physical bottlenecks

This is the strongest entry barrier in the batch: **no competitor can build the primary asset at all** on any timescale relevant to this decade's demand.

| Asset | Character | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Nuclear fleet | Largest in the US; **93.0% capacity factor** in Q2 | Licence renewal, refuelling capex, no new build possible |
| Calpine gas fleet | Dispatchable, acquired 2026 | Merchant exposure; one divestiture required and agreed |
| Retail supply | Leading US competitive supplier | Margin competition |
| Uprates | 30 MW at Dresden via the Walmart PPA | Incremental, not transformational |

Operating execution is excellent — 93.0% capacity factor across six outages including 86 planned refuelling days. Growth comes from **uprates**, which are cheap megawatts but small ones.

**The cost the thesis omits.** Refuelling, licence renewal and life-extension capital are permanent, large and non-discretionary. A nuclear fleet's EBITDA and its free cash flow are further apart than in almost any other asset class — and the figure is not in the available disclosure.

Constellation also agreed to sell the 606 MW Brazos Valley ERCOT gas plant for **$860m** — the last divestiture required under the Calpine regulatory commitments, and a useful external mark at ~$1.42m/MW on dispatchable capacity.

# Method

Each company was run through the same five-phase protocol:

1. **Pricing-power architecture** — where the pricing power comes from, and whether it is contractual, physical, regulatory or absent
2. **Asset base & physical bottlenecks** — what cannot be replicated, and how fast the bottleneck can be relieved
3. **Demand & monetisation structure** — every demand source classified `[Existing Contracted]` / `[Approved-Committed Pipeline]` / `[Unfunded Planned]` / `[Speculative Demand]`, the last earning **zero base-case weight**
4. **Capital structure & returns on capital** — who owns the cash flow, and at what leverage
5. **Valuation, stress tests & IC memo** — DCF, asset-base multiple, justified price-to-book and sum-of-the-parts, weighted per company, plus scenarios, IRR, non-consensus points and sell triggers

Every claim carries a taxonomy label — `[FACT]`, `[REGULATORY STATUTE]`, `[MANAGEMENT GUIDANCE]`, `[CONSENSUS]`, `[ANALYST ESTIMATE]` — with a source and an as-of date. Where a number could not be sourced it is declared a `[GAP]` rather than estimated silently.

Valuation arithmetic was computed by a deterministic engine, not asserted. Method weights were varied per company where the default was inappropriate, with the reason stated in each file rather than left implicit:

- **PPC** and **ADMIE Holding** exclude the discounted cash flow entirely — free cash flow is negative across the whole explicit window during a build, so a DCF would place effectively all value in the terminal value
- **Vistra** excludes both the price-earnings multiple and price-to-book — hedge mark-to-market makes GAAP earnings uninformative, and book equity is meaningless after USD 6.5bn of buybacks
- **Metlen** weights the sum-of-the-parts at 40% — the appropriate primary method for a five-part conglomerate

Three ratings were stated deliberately against the model, each with the reason recorded: **Constellation** (HOLD vs AVOID), **Metlen** (accumulate vs neutral), **ADMIE Holding** (BUY vs HOLD).
