# Phase 4 — Capital structure, balance-sheet risk & returns on capital
## Meta Platforms, Inc. (NASDAQ: META) · as of 2026-09-06

**The question:** can the balance sheet fund the plan, and does the business out-earn its cost of
capital?

---

## 4.1 The capital base

Meta has no regulated asset base, no allowed return and no rate case. The correct measure is invested
capital, and the correct test is the return on the *increment*, not on the stock.

a "net cash balance sheet" is a misleading description of this company.

reportedly been considered. Neither is the behaviour of a company that believes its funding is free.

## 4.3 Mandatory sensitivities

### (a) Interest rates +100bp / +200bp

Meta's existing debt is fixed rate and long dated, so a rate move does not touch the stock. It touches
new issuance — and, far more importantly for an equity holder, the discount rate.

| Shock | Transmission | FY2028E P&L effect | Effect on DCF fair value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **+100bp** | Applies only to the ~$50bn/yr of new issuance and the ~$100bn expected 2027–28 | **~−$1.0bn/yr** of pre-tax income by 2028 (~1% of operating income) | **~−17%.** WACC 9.5% → 10.5% cuts the base DCF from ~$501 to ~$416/share |
| **+200bp** | Same mechanism, two years of issuance | **~−$2.0bn/yr** by 2028 | **~−30%** |

**This is the most important sensitivity in the analysis and it does not run through the income
statement at all.** With ~87% of the base-case DCF value sitting in the terminal value, Meta's equity
is a long-duration asset. A 100bp move in the risk-free rate is worth roughly seventeen times more to
this equity than it is to this credit. The 10-year Treasury at **4.79%** (2026-09-04), its highest
since November 2023, is already doing that work — it is a large part of why the stock is down 15% in
a year while revenue grew 28%.

### (b) 10% move in the US dollar

Meta reports in USD. Roughly **56% of revenue is non-US** (Q2 2026: Europe $14.3bn, APAC $11.2bn, RoW
$8.5bn of $60.3bn regional revenue), while the cost base — data centres, GPUs, US payroll — is
overwhelmingly USD. This is an unusually clean asymmetry.

| Shock | Revenue effect | Cost effect | Operating income effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **USD −10% (dollar weakens)** | +$14.1bn on FY2026E revenue (+5.6%) | ~0 | **+$14.1bn, ~+17% of operating income** |
| **USD +10% (dollar strengthens)** | −$14.1bn (−5.6%) | ~0 | **−$14.1bn, ~−17% of operating income** |

[ANALYST ESTIMATE] Assumes full translation pass-through and no local-currency price response —
the upper bound of the effect. **A strengthening dollar is a materially under-discussed risk here:
it costs Meta roughly a sixth of operating income for a 10% move, with no offset.**

### (c) The input-cost shock that matters: a depreciation-life reversal

For Meta the input-cost shock is not fuel or wages. It is the **useful life of a GPU** — an accounting
estimate that is also an economic judgement, and the one input management most directly controls.

| Scenario | Server life | FY2028E D&A | Change vs base | Operating income effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current policy | 5.5 yrs | ~$55bn | — | — |
| **Life cut to 4.0 yrs** (obsolescence proves faster than assumed) | 4.0 yrs | ~$75bn | **+$20bn** | **−$20bn, ~−18% of FY2028E operating income** |
| Life extended to 7.0 yrs | 7.0 yrs | ~$43bn | −$12bn | +$12bn, ~+11% — **with zero cash effect** |

[ANALYST ESTIMATE] built on the cumulative 2025–28 IT capital base and Meta's disclosed 5.5-year policy
(effective 2025-01-01, which itself cut FY2025 depreciation by ~$2.9bn).

Sized to a real historical episode, the relevant precedent is **FY2022**, when an exogenous shock (ATT
plus an ad recession) hit revenue while the cost base was committed: revenue fell 1% and operating
income fell **38%** (from $46.75bn to $28.94bn). Meta's operating leverage works in both directions, and
in 2027 the committed cost base is three times larger than it was in 2022.

## 4.4 ROIC vs WACC — the only test that matters

method stated above; risk-free anchored to the 10-year Treasury in each year.

**Read this table before reading anything else in this report.** Meta has earned two to four times its
cost of capital every year for which data exists, including through a recession, a platform shock and a
$60bn cumulative Reality Labs loss. This is one of the highest-return capital bases in the listed
world. Any bear case that ignores it is not serious.

### The incremental record — where the problem is

Average ROIC measures a franchise built over twenty years. It cannot answer whether *this year's*
capital is earning. Incremental ROIC can.

```
ΔNOPAT / ΔInvested capital, at a 16% tax rate

2025 → 2026E     ΔNOPAT   = ($85.0bn − $83.28bn) × 0.84   =  $1.4bn
                 ΔIC      ≈ capex $137.5bn − D&A ~$35bn   ≈  $103bn
                 INCREMENTAL ROIC                          =  1.4%

2025 → 2028E     ΔNOPAT   = ($110bn − $83.28bn) × 0.84    =  $22.4bn
                 ΔIC      ≈ cumulative 2026-28 capex less D&A ≈ $350bn
                 INCREMENTAL ROIC                          =  6.4%

WACC                                                       =  9.5%
```

| Measure | Value | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Average ROIC (2025) | 36.9% | Extraordinary |
| Average ROIC (2028E, est.) | ~17–18% | Still excellent |
| **Incremental ROIC (2025→2026E)** | **1.4%** | **Far below WACC** |
| **Incremental ROIC (2025→2028E)** | **6.4%** | **Below WACC** |

**The precise finding, in one sentence: Meta's average ROIC remains excellent because the legacy
advertising franchise is extraordinary, while the incremental ROIC on the 2025–28 capital vintage is
1–6% against a 9.5% cost of capital. The company is consuming the excess returns of its franchise to
fund a programme that, on current evidence, earns below its cost of capital.**

That is precisely the failure mode the growth-≠-value guardrail exists to detect. It is invisible in
the average, invisible in the P/E, and visible only here.

### What it would take for the spread to turn durably positive

Solve for the operating income at which the 2025–28 vintage clears 9.5%:

```
Required ΔNOPAT   =  ΔIC $350bn × 9.5%                =  $33.3bn
Required Δ operating income  =  $33.3bn ÷ 0.84        =  $39.6bn
Required FY2028 operating income  =  $83.28bn + $39.6bn  =  $122.9bn
On FY2028E revenue of ~$348bn                         =  35.3% operating margin
```

| Condition | Required | Current | Plausible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2028 operating margin | **35.3%** | FY2025 41.4%; FY2026E 33.7% | **Yes — this is the base case.** It requires expense growth to decelerate below revenue growth from 2027, which happens automatically once capex plateaus and depreciation stops accelerating |
| Or: revenue per GW above $12.8bn | Capex per GW below $40bn, or gross margin above 80% | Unknown | Possible; unmeasurable on current disclosure |
| Or: a second revenue stream at $20bn+ | Meta AI subscriptions, agents, licensing | ~$2bn (messaging) | Not in the base case |

**Cyclical or structural?** This is a *self-inflicted, controllable* compression, not a cyclical one and
not a structural one. Nothing about Meta's competitive position, pricing power or demand has
deteriorated — Phases 1 and 3 establish the opposite. The spread compressed because management chose to
triple the capital base. It can therefore un-compress on the same decision, which is why the durable
positive spread is the base case rather than the bull case — and why the 2027 capex number is the
single most valuable disclosure in front of this stock.

## What this phase establishes

1. **The balance sheet can fund the plan, comfortably, for now.** AA−/Aa3, no near-term maturity wall,
   a ~$6.9bn FY2026 funding gap against $90.26bn of liquidity and a $25bn issuance already done. The
   constraint is the rating agencies' patience — Moody's models sub-1.0x debt/EBITDA, which implies
   only about $36bn of headroom, roughly three months of capex.
2. **Meta's average ROIC is one of the best in the listed world and has been for a decade** — 36.9% in
   2025 against a ~9.4% WACC. Any bear case that ignores this is not serious.
3. **Incremental ROIC on the 2025–28 capital vintage is 1.4–6.4% against a 9.5% WACC.** The franchise's
   excess returns are funding a programme that earns below its cost of capital.
4. **The spread turns durably positive at a 35.3% FY2028 operating margin** — versus 41.4% in 2025 and
   33.7% estimated for 2026. This is achievable and is the base case, but it requires the capex plateau,
   not a demand surprise.
5. **The dominant sensitivity is the discount rate, not the income statement.** A 100bp WACC move is
   worth ~17% of the equity value; the same move costs the credit ~$1bn a year. With the 10-year at
   4.79%, its highest since 2023, this is already the largest single driver of the 15% twelve-month
   de-rating.

## What it changes about the thesis

Phase 3 said the demand exists but the margin on it does not. Phase 4 says why, and how much of it is
reversible: **the franchise is earning 37% on capital and the new capital is earning 1–6%.** The
condition for repair is arithmetically specific — a 35.3% FY2028 operating margin — and it is within
management's control rather than the market's.

This converts the investment case from a quality judgement into a **timing judgement on one variable.**
Phase 5 prices that variable, and it must weight its methods accordingly: a DCF whose terminal value is
87% of the answer cannot carry the primary weight when the disputed input is the shape of the next
three years.
