### Defending the discount analytically

Meta trades at **18.2x** 2027 consensus earnings against a populated-peer median near **27.9x** — a
headline discount of roughly **35%**. Decomposed:

| Component of the gap | Points of P/E | Justified? |
|---|---|---|
| **Free cash flow at ~zero while peers convert** | ~15 pts | **Yes.** FY2026E FCF ≈ $0 on $252bn of revenue; the dividend is debt-funded |
| **Zero contracted revenue** vs Microsoft's and Oracle's backlogs | ~5 pts | **Yes.** A spot book deserves a lower multiple than a contracted one, mechanically |
| **Controlled company, no minority mechanism** | ~5 pts | **Yes.** 61% of votes in one hand, with buybacks already suspended |
| **Litigation and design-mandate overhang** | ~2 pts | **Yes**, though largely resolved in cash terms by the August settlement |
| **Unexplained residual** | **~3 pts (~11% of price)** | The actual opportunity |

**The headline discount is 35%. The unexplained residual is about 3 points of P/E — roughly 11%.**
That is the honest size of the peer-based opportunity, and it is not enough on its own to buy the stock.

## 5.2 Three scenarios

Each is a coherent state of the world with an explicit trigger set, consistent across Phases 1–4.

| | **Bear (30%)** | **Base (45%)** | **Bull (25%)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trigger set** | 2027 capex ≥$180bn; an ad-demand pause on the 2022 pattern; OpenAI/TikTok take 2–3 pts of incremental budget; youth-safety mandates cut teen impressions; USD strengthens | 2027 capex ~$165bn then plateaus; ad growth decelerates gracefully to mid-teens; design mandates cost ~1% of impressions; no second revenue stream at scale | 2027 capex ~$165bn then *falls*; ranking gains keep price per ad above +10%; business agents and Meta AI subscriptions reach $20bn+; buybacks resume; rates fall |
| Revenue 2027→2031 ($bn) | 275 → 355 | 300 → 490 | 315 → 555 |
| OCF margin | 50% → 50% | 55% → 58% | 56% → 62% |
| Capex ($bn) | 160 → 100 (forced retrenchment) | 165 → 175 (plateau) | 165 → 170 |
| **FCF 2027–31 ($bn)** | **−22.5, −6, 23.6, 56.6, 77.5** | **0, 17, 46, 80, 109** | **11.4, 42.5, 86, 132, 174** |
| WACC | **10.8%** | **9.5%** | **8.8%** |
| Terminal growth | 1.5% | 3.0% | 3.5% |
| ROE (for P/B) | 15% | 24% | 30% |
| EV/IC multiple | 3.0x | 4.3x | 5.5x |

**Why the WACC differs by scenario.** In the bear case a company burning cash at a $180bn capex
run-rate with a spot revenue book is a different credit and a different equity: rating pressure, a
higher equity risk premium and a wider spread. In the bull case a capex plateau plus resumed buybacks
plus a falling risk-free rate compresses it. A bear case run at the base WACC is not a stress test.

**Why 30/45/25 and not 25/50/25.** The bear weight is raised above symmetry for one evidenced reason:
management has raised capex guidance **twice in 2026** and **declined to guide 2027 at all**. The
observed behaviour of the decision-maker has been consistently toward more spending, not less. The bull
weight is held at 25% rather than cut, because the ad engine's performance (+27% revenue, +12% price,
+19% in Europe) is genuinely exceptional and because a capex plateau is entirely within one person's
control — it could be announced in a single sentence in January.

## 5.4 Investment committee memo

### Rating: **AVOID**

This is a **price call, not a quality call.** Meta is one of the finest businesses in the listed world —
36.9% ROIC, 27% revenue growth, 12% pricing power on a flat user base, a fortress credit at AA−/Aa3.
It is being offered at a price that requires the current capital programme to work, and the arithmetic
says the programme is, at today's run-rate, running to stand still.

### Fair value today

| | Bear | Base | Bull | Prob-weighted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Probability | 30% | 45% | 25% | |
| **Fair value / share** | **$231** | **$501** | **$1,003** | **$496** |
| vs $615.20 | −62% | −19% | +63% | **−19.3%** |

Single listing, USD; no ADR line. No FX conversion required.

### Three-year target and expected IRR (to September 2029, dividends included)

| | Bear | Base | Bull | Expected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3-year price target | $260 | $672 | $1,257 | $701 |
| Cumulative dividends | $6.3 | $6.6 | $6.9 | $6.6 |
| **IRR (CAGR)** | **−24.4%** | **+3.3%** | **+27.1%** | **+4.5%** |

**An expected IRR of 4.5% against a 10.0% cost of equity — and against a 4.79% risk-free rate — is
the whole case.** The investor is being asked to take mega-cap equity risk, a controlled-company
governance structure, a zero-contract revenue book and a $137bn annual capital commitment, for
approximately the return on a Treasury bill. That is what AVOID means here.

### Why the market is mispricing this — three non-consensus points

**1 · The "cheap 19x P/E" rests on an E the company's own guidance does not support.**
*Market believes:* Meta at 19.4x 2026 and 18.2x 2027 consensus EPS is the cheapest mega-cap AI asset.
*We believe:* the consensus EPS is not reconcilable to management's guidance. On ~$252bn of revenue and
guided expenses of $165–169bn, operating income is ~$85bn and EPS lands nearer **$27** — about 14%
below the $31.72 consensus, putting the real multiple at ~22.5x, not 19.4x.
*Evidence:* Phase 4 reconciliation of FY2026 expense guidance against the consensus figure; FY2025
operating income of $83.28bn against an FY2026E of ~$85bn on $51bn more revenue.

**2 · The capex hurdle grows faster than the revenue. Nobody is modelling the hurdle.**
*Market believes:* the risk is that AI capex doesn't pay off "eventually"; the ad business is safe.
*We believe:* the arithmetic is tighter and more immediate than that. At $12.8bn of required revenue per
gigawatt, a $165bn capital year adds ~$53bn to the annual revenue Meta must earn just to cover its cost
of capital — against actual annual revenue increments of $51–53bn. At the $180bn run-rate the 2027–28
plans imply, the hurdle grows ~$58bn a year. **The programme runs to stand still.**
*Evidence:* Phase 2 unit cost curve (§2.2) and Phase 3 §3.2; incremental ROIC of 1.4% (2025→26E) and
6.4% (2025→28E) against a 9.5% WACC in Phase 4 §4.4.

**3 · The consensus regulatory fear is pointed at the wrong regulator.**
*Market believes:* the DMA and EU data regulation are the structural threat to Meta's targeting.
*We believe:* Europe is Meta's **fastest-pricing region at +19% per ad** — the DMA has not bitten. The
regulatory instrument that actually threatens the model is the August 2026 state AG settlement, whose
design mandates (one-hour daily limits, nighttime blocks, age assurance) attack **impressions** — the
term that has been carrying 14 points of the 27% growth — and which appears in no sell-side model.
*Evidence:* Phase 1 §1.2 regional pricing table; the settlement's own terms, including the 30% tranche
contingent on YouTube and TikTok adopting equivalent measures.

### What the consensus is correctly terrified of — three structural risks

**1 · Committed cost against a spot revenue book.** ~$279bn of not-yet-commenced lease obligations and
a 5.5-year asset life sit behind revenue with **no take-or-pay, no minimum volume and no indexation**.
The precedent is exact and recent: in FY2022 revenue fell 1% and operating income fell **38%**. The
committed 2027 cost base is roughly three times the 2022 base. A single soft advertising year now does
structurally more damage than it did then, and Meta cannot cut the leases.

**2 · The compute has no second buyer.** Meta open-weighted Muse Spark 1.2 and open-sourced Muse
Glimmer, deliberately forgoing any licensing annuity. Every dollar of the $137bn must be recovered
through advertising and first-party products — in a year when OpenAI's ad run-rate went from ~$100m to
~$1bn in six months and TikTok's US joint venture became a fully-funded, US-governed competitor. A
regulated utility with stranded assets gets cost recovery. Meta gets a 5.5-year write-off.

**3 · No one can stop it.** 61% of the votes sit with one holder who has, in 2026 alone, raised capex
guidance twice, suspended a $25bn buyback, funded the dividend with debt, and reportedly considered an
equity offering. There is no board mechanism, no proxy mechanism and no activist mechanism. If the
programme doubles again, minority shareholders will read about it.

### Trigger-to-sell conditions (thesis-invalidating, measurable)

*The rating is already AVOID; these are the thresholds that would move it to an outright short-candidate
review and that a holder should treat as exit conditions:*

1. **FY2027 capex guided above $180bn.**
2. **Incremental operating margin below 10% for two consecutive quarters** (Δ operating income ÷ Δ revenue, y/y).
3. **Any primary equity issuance** to fund the capital programme.
4. **Total debt/EBITDA above 1.5x for two consecutive quarters**, or a downgrade below AA−/Aa3.
5. **Price per ad growth below +5% y/y for two consecutive quarters** — the pricing mechanism failing.
6. **Family DAP declining year-on-year in any quarter.**

### Trigger-to-buy conditions (would flip this to TACTICAL BUY)

The thesis is falsifiable in the other direction, and precisely:

1. **FY2027 capex guided at or below $150bn** — reduces the annual hurdle by ~$10bn and adds roughly
   $120/share to the base case on its own.
2. **Two consecutive quarters of incremental operating margin above 20%.**
3. **Buyback resumption at ≥$10bn annualised.**
4. **A disclosed AI or business-messaging revenue line at ≥$10bn run-rate.**

Any two of these together take the probability-weighted fair value above the current price. **This is
not a permanent bear case. It is a bear case with a date on it — late January 2027.**

## What this phase establishes

1. **Triangulated fair value is $496 against a price of $615.20 — a 19.3% overvaluation** — on a
   30/45/25 scenario weighting, with methods weighted SOTP 30%, DCF 35%, EV/IC 25%, P/B 10%.
2. **Expected three-year IRR is 4.5% against a 10.0% cost of equity** and a 4.79% risk-free rate.
   That, not the fair-value gap, is the decisive number.
3. **The headline 35% peer discount is roughly 32 points explained** — by zero free cash flow, a
   zero-contract revenue book, controlled-company governance and litigation — leaving an unexplained
   residual of only ~3 points of P/E, about 11%.
4. **Roughly 87% of DCF value is terminal in every scenario**, which is why the DCF cannot carry the
   primary weight and why the SOTP — which separates the proven franchise from the unproven programme —
   is weighted alongside it.
5. **The dispersion is enormous and honest: $231 bear to $1,003 bull.** This is not a high-conviction
   valuation; it is a high-conviction *statement about which variable matters*, and that variable gets
   disclosed in late January 2027.

## What it changes about the thesis

Nothing in Phases 1–4 is contradicted here; Phase 5 prices it. The franchise is exceptional and the
programme is not yet earning. At $615.20 the market is paying for the franchise **and** marking the
programme at roughly half of cost — which is a defensible thing to do, and still leaves the expected
return below the cost of equity.

The disciplined conclusion is to own the business at a price that does not require the programme to
work. That price, on this arithmetic, is approximately **$500**. Below it, most of the outcomes are
acceptable. Above it, the investor is underwriting a capital-allocation decision made by someone who
has told them, twice this year, that the number is going up.
