## Phase 1 — Pricing-power architecture

Group profitability has **roughly doubled in a single year**: profit before special items went from €2,355m (6.0% margin) in FY2025 to a guided 10–12% for FY2026, with management trending to the upper end. Q3 alone printed **14.2%** (€1,623m), more than triple the prior-year quarter.

Pricing power here is a **delivery-slot auction, not a price list**. Competition in grid equipment has shifted from price to manufacturing capacity and guaranteed delivery dates. That is the purest form of scarcity rent — and it is exactly as durable as the scarcity, and no more.

**The central tension, and it is GE Vernova's tension verbatim.** Siemens Energy plans ~€6bn of investment by 2028, about a third of it — some **€2bn** — going to transformer and gas-insulated switchgear plants, expanding that capacity **~50% by 2030**. *The company is spending €2bn to relieve the shortage it is being valued on.* Hitachi Energy is committing >$1bn to the same thing; GE Vernova is expanding through Prolec.

**One point in the company's favour the thesis omits.** Siemens Gamesa lost €260m before special items in H1 FY2026 against €932m a year earlier, narrowed to a €44m quarterly loss, and is guided to **break even in FY2026**. GE Vernova's wind segment is still contracting at −40% orders. This is the single largest operating difference between the two companies.

## Phase 2 — Asset base & the backlog clock

| | Backlog | Annual revenue | Queue length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Siemens Energy | €162bn | ~€45bn FY2026E | **~3.6 years** |
| GE Vernova (turbines only) | 116 GW | 20 GW output | 5.8 years |

A 3.6-year queue supports premium pricing — for fewer years than a 5.8-year one. It is still lengthening: **Q3 book-to-bill was ~1.57×** (€17.9bn orders on €11.4bn revenue), adding roughly €26bn of backlog in a quarter.

**The decisive structural point in this whole batch.** How fast can the bottleneck be relieved?

| Constraint | Time to add capacity | Capital | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large power transformers | **~3 years per plant** | $150–460m | Fundable and buildable; three firms are doing it |
| Gas-insulated switchgear | ~2–3 years | Part of the €6bn plan | +50% capacity targeted by 2030 |
| Heavy-duty gas turbines | Longer, specialised | Higher | The stickier bottleneck |
| **Skilled labour and GOES supply** | Uncertain | Not the binding cost | **The real constraint** |

Siemens Energy's first US large power transformer plant — $150m, Charlotte — begins production in **early 2027**. A further $185m, 550,000 sq ft plant in Jackson County, Georgia breaks ground in November 2026 with ~3 years to full buildout and **more than 1,400 new hires** required.

This is a manufacturing constraint, not an irreplaceable asset. **Constellation's nuclear fleet cannot be rebuilt at any price. Siemens Energy's asset base is being rebuilt by its competitors right now.**

**The finding that drives this dossier**, and it repeats GE Vernova's exactly:

| Measure | FY2026E | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Free cash flow pre-tax | ~€8.0bn | Guided |
| Profit before special items | ~€5.3bn | FCF is **~1.5×** this |
| Net income | ~€4.0bn | FCF is **~2.0×** this |
| Q3 book-to-bill | **1.57×** | The advance pool is still filling |
| **Underlying FCF at 1.0× book-to-bill** | **~€5.5bn pre-tax** | A gap of roughly **€2.5bn a year** |

Management attributes the increase explicitly to **customer advance payments and reservation fees** on the strong order intake. This is not hidden and it is not an accounting doubt — it is arithmetic. Customers are prepaying against orders faster than the company converts them to revenue. When book-to-bill approaches 1.0×, the advance pool stops growing and free cash flow converges toward profit plus depreciation.

**The difference from GE Vernova is one of degree, and it favours Siemens Energy.** GEV guides FCF at roughly **2× adjusted EBITDA**; ENR guides it at roughly **1.5× profit before special items**. The distortion is present in both and materially smaller here.

## Phase 5 — Valuation & IC memo

The single decision that drives this valuation: **the DCF uses underlying free cash flow, not the guided €8bn.** Using the headline figure would raise fair value by roughly 40% and would be valuing customer prepayments as though they were earnings.

| Method | Value/share | vs €143.68 | Weight | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA @ 17× | €145 | **+1%** | 40% | Low |
| DCF *(underlying FCF)* | €139 | −3% | 35% | Low — 77% terminal value |
| Sum of the parts | €120 | −16% | 25% | Low |
| **Blended base case** | **€137** | **−5%** | 100% | Prob-weighted **€139** |
| Sell-side average | €196 | +36% | cross-check | Capitalises *guided* FCF |

All three methods cluster within 16% of each other — a much tighter spread than at Constellation. **The uncertainty here is about the future, not about the inputs.**

| Scenario | Prob. | Trigger set | FV | 3y IRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | 25% | Book-to-bill <1.0×; advances unwind; capacity expansion compresses Grid margins toward 14% | €102 | −10.1% |
| Base | 50% | Margins hold near the upper guided range; book-to-bill normalises gradually; buyback proceeds | €137 | +7.1% |
| Bull | 25% | Grid margins exceed 20%; the shortage persists past 2030; Gamesa turns profitable | €179 | +23.1% |

Expected 3-year IRR of ~6.8% sits at or just below a reasonable cost of equity. **That is what a fairly valued equity looks like.**

**Reverse valuation.** To justify €122bn of equity at a fair 15× EBITDA the business needs **~€8.7bn of EBITDA** against ~€6.8bn guided this year — a 28% increase, achievable by 2028 on the current trajectory *if scarcity pricing survives the capacity build*. The price is a specific bet on exactly the Phase 1 tension — and, unlike GE Vernova, it is not an extravagant one.

**Why this sits 29% below consensus, in one line.** Consensus appears to capitalise the guided €8bn of free cash flow. This analysis capitalises ~€5.5bn of underlying free cash flow. That single adjustment accounts for almost the whole difference. It is a disagreement about **one number**, not about the business.

### Three non-consensus points

1. **~€2.5bn/yr of the free cash flow is customer prepayment, and management says so.** Underlying FCF is closer to €5.5bn pre-tax. This is worth ~40% of fair value and accounts for the entire gap to the €196 consensus target.
2. **The company is spending €2bn to end the scarcity it is being valued on.** A ~50% expansion of transformer and GIS capacity by 2030, with Hitachi and GE Vernova doing the same. First plants produce from early 2027. This is a manufacturing bottleneck with a three-year relief cycle, not an irreplaceable asset.
3. **GE Vernova is the same trade at ~2.4× the EBITDA multiple.** Same demand drivers, comparable net cash balance sheet, a July credit upgrade, and a wind business guided to break-even where GEV's is still contracting. The dispersion between ~17× and ~40× is not explained by anything found in either dossier.

### What the bulls are correctly confident about

1. **The scarcity may last longer than three years.** Lead times are set by skilled labour and grain-oriented electrical steel as much as by buildings — the Georgia plant alone needs 1,400+ hires. *Capacity announcements are not capacity.* This is the strongest argument against fading the premium inside the forecast window.
2. **Management has beaten its own plan twice.** Grid Technologies hit its FY2028 target in FY2026. A team that keeps beating targets may be guiding conservatively rather than peaking.
3. **The advance effect does not reverse — it stops growing.** Free cash flow converges *to* underlying rather than falling below it. The bear case here is a plateau, not an unwind, which is meaningfully gentler than GE Vernova's.

### Trigger-to-sell
- Book-to-bill below 1.0× for two consecutive quarters
- FCF pre-tax below profit before special items for two consecutive quarters — confirms the advance reversal
- Grid Technologies margin before special items below 16% in any quarter
- Group margin before special items failing to exceed 12% in FY2027
- Industry transformer lead times falling below 80 weeks
- Siemens Gamesa returning to a loss after the guided break-even
- Order intake growth below zero YoY for two consecutive quarters

### Re-entry test
Below **~€120** the shares sit under the sum-of-the-parts floor with a base-case IRR above 12% — that justifies buying. Above **~€180** the price requires the scarcity premium to survive the capacity build intact, which Phase 2 argues against. Between those points the shares are fairly priced; hold and watch **book-to-bill**, the single number that tells you when the cash flow story changes.

### Largest gaps
1. **The change in contract liabilities and customer advances** — would settle whether the DCF uses €8bn or €5.5bn. Worth ~40% of fair value. Nothing else matters as much.
2. Backlog margin profile and cancellation terms
3. Aggregate industry transformer capacity additions — decides whether the rent ends 2029 or 2033
4. Reported net cash, D&A and shareholders' equity
