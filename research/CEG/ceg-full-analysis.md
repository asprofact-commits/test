The mechanism in the thesis is real, growing and long-dated. Its **scope** is about a third of what the sentence implies.

## Phase 1 — Pricing-power architecture

Pricing power here is contractual and physical at once. The product cannot be built quickly, cannot be sited freely, and cannot be replicated by renewables-plus-storage at the reliability level a hyperscaler contracts for. That is why a premium exists at all.

But the equity owns the whole fleet, not just the contracted part:

| Output | Basis | Duration | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~30% of clean baseload | Long-term PPAs, investment-grade counterparties | **18.5 years avg** | Genuinely premium, genuinely locked |
| **~70% of clean baseload** | **Merchant / wholesale** | Spot to short | **Exposed to power prices and PJM capacity auctions** |
| Calpine gas fleet | Dispatchable, merchant | Short | Cyclical; hedges the nuclear position |
| Retail supply | Customer contracts | Short–medium | Margin-driven, competitive |

**Regulatory dependence is unusually high and cuts both ways.** Licence extensions, PJM capacity auction design, state clean-energy credits and federal PTCs all move realised price, and none are under company control. Higher PJM capacity prices were a *cited driver of the Q2 beat* — an administrative auction outcome, not a commercial win.

## Phase 2 — Asset base & physical bottlenecks

This is the strongest entry barrier in the batch: **no competitor can build the primary asset at all** on any timescale relevant to this decade's demand.

| Asset | Character | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Nuclear fleet | Largest in the US; **93.0% capacity factor** in Q2 | Licence renewal, refuelling capex, no new build possible |
| Calpine gas fleet | Dispatchable, acquired 2026 | Merchant exposure; one divestiture required and agreed |
| Retail supply | Leading US competitive supplier | Margin competition |
| Uprates | 30 MW at Dresden via the Walmart PPA | Incremental, not transformational |

Operating execution is excellent — 93.0% capacity factor across six outages including 86 planned refuelling days. Growth comes from **uprates**, which are cheap megawatts but small ones.

**The cost the thesis omits.** Refuelling, licence renewal and life-extension capital are permanent, large and non-discretionary. A nuclear fleet's EBITDA and its free cash flow are further apart than in almost any other asset class — and the figure is not in the available disclosure.

Constellation also agreed to sell the 606 MW Brazos Valley ERCOT gas plant for **$860m** — the last divestiture required under the Calpine regulatory commitments, and a useful external mark at ~$1.42m/MW on dispatchable capacity.

### Why the rating is HOLD and the model says AVOID

The model returns **AVOID at −11.2%**. I am stating **HOLD**, and the reason is arithmetic, not sentiment: shares outstanding and net debt are both unsourced estimates, and a 15% error in either moves fair value by more than the 11% gap. In the Apple dossier a −32% gap sat far outside any error bar and the rating moved to the arithmetic. **Here the arithmetic is inside its own noise.** The 23.6× multiple — the one figure independent of those estimates — does not indicate an expensive stock.

### Three non-consensus points

1. **The contracted-premium story covers under a third of the fleet.** ~70% of clean baseload is merchant, exposed to wholesale prices and PJM auctions. The named Q2 counterparty was Walmart, not a hyperscaler. The mechanism is real; the description overstates its current scope by roughly three times.
2. **Two of the three Q2 earnings drivers are not repeatable.** Calpine is an acquisition; higher PJM capacity prices are an administrative outcome. Only improved customer margins are a commercial achievement.
3. **This is a leveraged merchant generator, not a contracted infrastructure asset.** ~$30bn estimated net debt, two-thirds merchant earnings, and a permanent non-discretionary nuclear sustaining-capital requirement.

### What the bulls are correctly confident about

1. **The asset base genuinely cannot be replicated.** No competitor can build a US nuclear fleet on any relevant timescale. Scarcity of that kind does not usually get cheaper — the strongest argument against this rating, and a good one.
2. **The contracting rate is accelerating.** 920 MW in one quarter at 18.5 years. If the remaining 70% contracts on similar terms, the merchant discount applied here is wrong.
3. **31% has already come out of the price.** Much of the AI-power premium still embedded in GE Vernova has already left this equity, and the base-case IRR is positive.

### Trigger-to-sell
- Nuclear PPA signing rate below 300 MW/quarter for two consecutive quarters
- Nuclear capacity factor below 90% for two consecutive quarters
- PJM capacity prices clearing materially below the level assumed in guidance
- FY2027 adjusted operating EPS guided below $12.00
- Net debt / EBITDA above 4.0× on reported figures once available
- Any extended unplanned outage at a large nuclear unit

### Re-entry test
Given that the fair-value estimate is less reliable than the 23.6× multiple, the useful test is **evidentiary rather than a price**: two further quarters at the current PPA signing rate would move the contracted share materially above 30% and justify a higher multiple on better grounds than this model can establish. The first priority is simply to obtain the 10-Q and replace the estimated share count and net debt with real ones.

### Largest gaps
1. **Shares outstanding and net debt post-Calpine** — both estimated; together they determine every per-share figure. The largest gap in any dossier in this series.
2. **Realised price premium in the nuclear PPAs** — the core thesis claim, and it is confidential.
3. **Merchant hedging position across the ~70% uncontracted baseload** — the largest earnings sensitivity in the company.
4. **Nuclear sustaining capital** — determines FCF conversion, and therefore the DCF that carries 35% weight.
