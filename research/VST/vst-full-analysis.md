## Phase 1 — Pricing-power architecture

The contracts are real and they are the best here: **three 20-year Meta PPAs unlocking ~2.6 GW** including uprates, and a **20-year AWS agreement for up to 1,200 MW at Comanche Peak** that more than triples the plant's contracted output, ramping from late 2027 through 2032. Twenty-year duration against Constellation's 18.5-year average, and both counterparties are the hyperscalers the thesis names — where Constellation's disclosed counterparty last quarter was Walmart and PPC has only a shortlist.

**Then the correction that decides the dossier:**

| Fuel | Capacity | Share of fleet | Contract status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Natural gas | **27 GW** | 61% | Merchant, ERCOT-weighted |
| **Coal** | **8.7 GW** | 20% | Merchant; retirement and carbon risk |
| Nuclear | 6.5 GW | 15% | ~3.8 GW under 20-year hyperscaler PPAs |
| Solar & storage | 1.3 GW | 3% | Mixed |
| **TOTAL** | **44 GW** | 100% | **~8.6% under hyperscaler PPA** |

The PPAs are excellent and they cover the *smallest* fuel in the fleet. The earnings come from the other 91%.

**The market has already demonstrated it.** Shares are down ~15% YTD and ~30% over twelve months against an S&P up ~13%, near the bottom of a 52-week range whose high was ~$219.70. Management named the cause on the Q2 call: **softer ERCOT forward prices.** The share price tracks the price of the electricity Vistra sells, not the quantity the market expects to need.

Two things the thesis omits entirely: **8.7 GW of coal** — a third more than the nuclear fleet, carrying the retirement, carbon and compliance risk — and the genuine pricing power in **retail**, ~5m customers across 20 states including nearly a third of Texas, which is a structural hedge pure merchant generators lack.

## Phase 2 — Asset base: barriers ranked

| Asset | Capacity | Barrier | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nuclear | 6.5 GW | **Very high** — cannot be rebuilt | Why hyperscalers sign 20-year terms |
| Contracted uprates | 433 MW | High — licensed sites only | Growth without permitting or merchant risk |
| Gas | 27 GW | Medium — 3–4 yrs to build, *but the turbine queue is full* | The earnings engine; scarcity temporary but real |
| **Coal** | 8.7 GW | **None** — the barrier is to *staying open* | Retirement and compliance liability |

*The asset with the strongest barrier is 15% of the fleet. The asset with none is 20%.*

The **433 MW of contracted uprates** at Perry, Davis-Besse and Beaver Valley is the most attractive structure in this batch: the customer funds capacity at an already-licensed nuclear site and contracts the output for two decades — capacity addition without permitting, siting or merchant risk.

**The turbine bottleneck cuts both ways, and this is the strongest argument for the equity.** Vistra buys from GE Vernova and Siemens Energy for growth — the wrong side. But if new gas capacity cannot be delivered for five years *because* those queues are 116 GW and ~70 GW deep, then existing dispatchable capacity in a load-growing market is scarcer than the current forward curve implies. This batch supplies the evidence for its own bull case.

That is a *fair* multiple for the mix — which is why the honest answer is that this equity is now **approximately correctly priced for its risk, having been substantially overpriced twelve months ago.**

### Three non-consensus points

1. **The contracts are real and cover 8.6% of the fleet.** 27 GW merchant gas, 8.7 GW coal, 6.5 GW nuclear. The stock fell ~30% because ERCOT forwards softened — the market demonstrating that the volatility was not bypassed. Management said so themselves.
2. **The second-largest fuel is coal, and nobody mentions it.** 8.7 GW, a third more than nuclear, with retirement obligations and carbon exposure no multiple here captures.
3. **Reported revenue and earnings are unusable for this company.** A 30% revenue miss alongside +31% EBITDA alongside $305m of GAAP net income — all true, all hedge marks. Hence no P/E, and valuation on cash.

### What the bulls are correctly confident about

1. **The cash yield is exceptional and real.** ~$4.3bn FCF-before-growth on ~$47.5bn is ~9.1%, and ~$6.5bn of buybacks retired ~30% of the shares. **An investor is paid very well to wait for the PPAs to ramp** — the strongest argument against a negative view.
2. **The turbine bottleneck protects the merchant fleet.** If no meaningful new ERCOT capacity can arrive for five years, existing dispatchable plant in a load-growing market is scarcer than the curve implies.
3. **20 analysts rate it Strong Buy at an average $217**, and this sits 39% below. When the entire covering universe is on the other side, the base rate favours them — though a $106–$305 range suggests they aren't confident either.

### Trigger-to-sell
- ERCOT forwards for 2028–29 falling further on two consecutive quarterly disclosures
- FY2027 adjusted EBITDA guided below $6.8bn
- Following-year hedge ratios disclosed below 80%
- Net debt/EBITDA above 3.0×
- Buyback suspended or the authorisation not renewed beyond the remaining $1.2bn
- Any delay to the AWS Comanche Peak ramp scheduled from late 2027
- A material coal retirement charge or accelerated closure mandate

### Re-entry test
Below **~$110** the shares offer a base-case IRR above 15% with a cash yield above 11% — a full position in a security this geared. At $139.80, hold and collect the yield. The evidentiary test is not a price but a curve: **two consecutive quarters in which ERCOT forwards for 2028–29 stop falling** would remove the specific thing that broke this equity, and the PPA ramp from late 2027 does the rest.

### Largest gaps
1. **Reported net debt** — derived, at ~39% of market cap; it gears every output.
2. **Growth capex** — guidance is FCF *before* growth; the DCF needs the after-growth figure.
3. **EBITDA by fuel** — carries 30% of the weighting and is the central analytical point here.
4. **Hedge ratios for 2027, 2029, 2030** — only the ~72% 2028 figure is available.
5. **Coal asset retirement obligations.**
