# Mastercard — Phase 4: Capital structure & returns on capital

**Mastercard Incorporated** · NYSE: MA · $580.00 · ~$507bn · 2026-09-05

> **Evidence note.** No primary filing was retrievable. **Net debt of ~$8bn is an analyst estimate**, not a sourced figure — at ~1.6% of market cap it moves per-share value by about $9, so the conclusion does not turn on it. The banking profile does not apply: no loan book, no credit risk, no CET1.

## There is no capital structure question

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| FY2025 free cash flow | **~$17.2bn** (~52% of revenue) |
| FY2026E free cash flow | ~$19.5bn (**~3.8% yield**) |
| Net debt | ~$8bn *(estimate)* — under half a year of FCF |
| Buyback authorised Dec 2025 | **$14bn** (~2.8% of market cap), plus $4.2bn remaining prior |
| Dividend | $0.87/qtr, **+14%** → ~0.58% yield |
| FY2025 operating margin | **57.6%**, +230bp |

**The buyback comparison across this series matters.** Mastercard repurchases at ~29x forward earnings while compounding FCF in the low-to-mid teens — neither the value destruction of Apple's programme at **37x**, nor the retreat of Alphabet's **halt**.

Return on invested capital is extraordinarily high and far exceeds the cost of capital, but the precise ratio is **not economically meaningful**: buybacks have shrunk book equity and the network was never capitalised. Same treatment as Apple, for the same reason.

## Sensitivities, ranked by what actually matters

| Shock | Effect | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Consumer spending / cross-border travel** | Direct and large | Revenue is a toll on volume; cross-border is the highest-rate line |
| **US dollar strength** | Material | Guidance is given currency-neutral precisely for this reason |
| **Premium card mix** | Potentially large, **newly live** | Honor-all-cards ends; merchants may decline top-tier cards |
| Rail substitution | Structural, unmeasured | Attacks cross-border first |
| Rates +200bp | ~$160m pre-tax, **<1%** of operating income | Minimal net debt |

The conventional balance-sheet sensitivities are irrelevant. Everything that matters is on the volume and mix side of the toll.

## The one real balance-sheet item

The $38bn settlement is shared with Visa and substantially covered by prior accruals and the long-standing litigation escrow — but the split and any incremental charge are **not established here**. It is the only genuine balance-sheet unknown in this dossier.

## The valuation question in one sentence

A **3.8% free cash flow yield** against a 10-year Treasury in the low fours means the equity is priced close to a risk-free yield **with mid-teens growth attached**.

## What this phase establishes
1. No capital structure question — ~$8bn net debt against ~$19.5bn of FCF.
2. Returns vastly exceed cost of capital; the ratio itself is not meaningful.
3. The buyback is executed at a defensible multiple, unlike Apple's.
4. Rate sensitivity is under 1% of operating income; currency and spending are what matter.
5. 3.8% FCF yield close to the risk-free rate — the whole valuation question.

## Could not establish
- Actual net debt, cash and investments (estimated $8bn)
- Mastercard's share of the settlement and any incremental charge
- Cross-border revenue as a share of total
- Sourced WACC components — **the DCF carries 50% of the weight here**

## What it changes
Removes every balance-sheet and returns concern, leaving a clean valuation problem. Because the cash flows are unusually stable, **the DCF becomes the most trustworthy instrument for this company** — the reverse of the Alphabet and SpaceX dossiers, and the reason its weight is raised to 50%.
