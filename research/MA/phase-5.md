# Investment Committee Memo

**Rating: `HOLD / NEUTRAL`** — model and analyst agree, for the first time in this series with a *positive* gap.

Fair value ~**$618** against **$580**, about **+6.5%**. That is a **modest discount, not a mispricing**: a 100bp WACC change moves the DCF by roughly 20% at these growth rates, so the gap is well inside the error bars. This is a quality judgement with a fair price, not an identified opportunity.

## Why the market is mispricing this — 3 non-consensus points

1. **Market believes:** the $38bn settlement is a manageable 0.1pp interchange cut.
   **We believe:** the rate cut is trivial. The substantive concession is the **end of honor-all-cards**, which for the first time lets merchants decline premium, rewards and commercial cards — the highest-interchange tier. That attacks the **mix, not the rate**, and cannot be sized publicly because volume by tier isn't broken out.
   *Evidence — Phase 1 settlement terms; Phase 5 gap list.*

2. **Market believes:** stablecoins are an existential threat.
   **We believe:** structurally credible, correctly aimed at cross-border, and **so far invisible** — Q2 revenue +14% with cross-border cited as a driver. Meanwhile Mastercard is buying the substitute for **~10% of one year's free cash flow**. Owning the rail isn't keeping the toll, but the option is nearly free relative to what it hedges.
   *Evidence — Phases 1–2: Q2 results against the substitution thesis; BVNK terms.*

3. **Market believes:** Mastercard is the expensive network and deserves its premium.
   **We believe:** **the premium has largely gone** — ~25× for Visa against ~27–28× for Mastercard, a 9–12% gap compressed from the historical relationship. You are paying much less of a relative premium than the reputation implies.
   *Evidence — Phase 5: sourced forward multiples, September 2026.*

## What the consensus is correctly terrified of

1. **The end of honor-all-cards is the first change in decades that touches mix rather than rate — and nobody, including this analysis, can size it.** It affects the most profitable part of the revenue base, it is newly live, and the disclosure needed to quantify it does not exist. That deserves genuine humility.
2. **Substitution attacks cross-border first** — simultaneously the highest-rate line and the current growth engine. It doesn't require a better network; it makes the network unnecessary for a flow.
3. **A 3.8% FCF yield against a low-four risk-free rate leaves no margin for a growth disappointment**, and the political direction of travel on interchange has been one-way for thirty years.

## Trigger-to-sell

- Net revenue growth **below 8%** YoY for two consecutive quarters
- VAS growth **below 12%** YoY for two consecutive quarters
- Cross-border volume growth **negative** in any quarter absent a travel shock
- Operating margin **below 54%** for two consecutive quarters
- **Any disclosed measurable volume migration** to stablecoin or A2A rails
- Forward P/E **above 36×** without an upgrade to the cash-flow path

## Re-entry

Fair value ~$618; the base case supports paying up to that. A 15% margin of safety implies adding below ~**$525** — inside the 52-week range, so a realistic entry rather than a theoretical one. With expected 3-year IRR of ~**+8.8%** and the tightest, most positively skewed distribution in this series, an existing holder has **no valuation reason to sell**.

**This is the one name of the four where the answer to "should I own this" is closer to yes than no.**

## What this phase could not establish

1. **Volume carried on premium and rewards cards** — the single most important number missing; it would size the honor-all-cards exposure
2. Cross-border revenue as a share of total — drives the bear case
3. Sourced WACC components — the DCF carries 50% weight, and a 100bp error moves it ~20%
4. Value-added services margin — 41% of revenue, undisclosed profitability, 20% of the valuation weight
5. Mastercard's share of the settlement and any incremental charge
