# Alphabet — Phase 4: Capital structure & returns on capital

**Alphabet Inc.** · NASDAQ: GOOGL · $338.72 · 2026-09-05

> **Evidence note.** No primary filing was retrievable — every external domain is blocked by this environment's egress proxy. Findings rest on search-result summaries.

## The finding that changes the whole dossier

Alphabet's Q2 2026 other income was a **net gain of $98.0bn**, primarily unrealised gains on equity securities in **SpaceX and one private company** — up $95.3bn year on year.

Alphabet disclosed the reconciliation itself. The $99.0bn net gain increased:

| | Effect |
|---|---|
| Tax provision | +$21.9bn |
| Net income | **+$77.1bn** |
| Diluted EPS | **+$6.26** |

So reported Q2 EPS of **$9.11** is **$2.85 of operating earnings and $6.26 of paper gain**.

That $2.85 closely matches the **$2.88 analyst consensus** — the sell-side was forecasting operating earnings while the headline printed a 294% EPS increase.

### Therefore the "cheapest megacap" claim is arithmetically wrong

| Measure | Reported | Clean |
|---|---|---|
| Q2 2026 EPS | $9.11 | **$2.85** |
| Q2 2026 net income | ~$112bn | ~$35bn |
| **Price / earnings** | **~17.0×** | **~29.8×** |

The most common reason given for owning this stock does not survive checking. The distortion is in the market's summary statistic, not in Alphabet's reporting.

## The capital structure inverted in a single year

| Policy | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Share repurchases | $28.3bn | **$0** | Halted ($69.5bn still authorised) |
| Equity issuance | None | **$84.75bn raise** | Largest in US corporate history; $10bn Berkshire anchor |
| Long-term debt | ~$49bn | **~$98bn** | Roughly doubled |
| Dividend | Initiated 2024 | $0.22/qtr, +5% | Maintained *while issuing stock* |
| Free cash flow | Strongly positive | **−$5.9bn in Q2** | First negative since the 2004 IPO |

Every line moved the same direction at once. A company that returned ~$70bn a year to shareholders is now raising debt and equity to fund capex — **the way a utility funds a grid buildout, not the way a software company funds growth**.

There is no solvency question: ~$142bn net cash, ~$170bn of operating income. There is now a genuine **funding** question, and the answer is selling stock while paying a dividend.

## Returns on capital

**Cannot be measured meaningfully mid-cycle.** The denominator is being rebuilt at ~$200bn a year and the assets are not yet generating full revenue, so any current ROIC reading is an artefact of timing.

That is not a neutral observation: it means **the central claim of the bull case is currently unfalsifiable**.

## Rate sensitivity has reversed

On ~$142bn net cash, +100bp still adds ~$1.4bn pre-tax. But with debt doubled and further issuance planned, the marginal effect of higher rates is now a **rising funding cost on a multi-year borrowing programme**, not a windfall on idle cash.

## What this phase establishes
1. The headline ~17× P/E is really ~29.8× on clean operating earnings.
2. Alphabet disclosed this itself — the market's shorthand is what misleads.
3. The capital structure inverted completely in one year.
4. No solvency risk; a real funding question.
5. ROIC is unmeasurable for several years, so the bull case cannot yet be tested.

## Could not establish
1. **Depreciation policy on compute assets** — largest determinant of clean earnings
2. Whether the $84.75bn raise is inside the reported cash balance and share count (~2% dilution either way)
3. Incremental return on the capex programme
4. FY2026 operating cash flow

## What it changes
Phases 1–3 built a strong operating case. Phase 4 establishes that **the multiple the market quotes is roughly half the real one**, and that the company now funds itself like a capital-intensive utility. Phase 5 must work on clean earnings and weight the DCF down.
