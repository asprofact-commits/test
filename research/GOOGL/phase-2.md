## An asset-light company became an infrastructure company in eighteen months

| Period | Capex | Change |
|---|---|---|
| FY2025 actual | $91.4bn | — |
| Q1 2026 | $35.7bn | — |
| Q2 2026 | **$44.9bn** | 2× YoY |
| FY2026 guidance | **$195–205bn** | **+118%** on FY2025 |
| FY2027 consensus | **~$257bn** | +28% again |

Guidance was *raised* mid-year from $180–190bn, and the CFO warned 2027 will rise "significantly" further. **There is no guided peak.** Two years ago this was software economics; it now spends like a national grid operator.

## But unlike the rest of the cohort, it can show where the money converts

| Asset | Evidence | Return signal |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud capacity | Backlog **$514bn**; revenue +82% | Operating margin **20.7% → 35.6%** |
| TPU silicon | External sales begun; multi-GW agreements | New revenue line, margin undisclosed |
| Gemini | 662m users; traffic share 5.7%→27.7% | Defends Search rather than billing directly |
| Search infrastructure | Search +17% | No visible cannibalisation |

**The bottleneck is capacity, not demand** — the good version of a constraint. It justifies the spending, but it also means the spending cannot stop while $514bn of backlog is unbuilt.

## The opposite side of Apple's trade

Apple rents frontier AI for **~$1bn a year**. Alphabet is spending **~$200bn a year** building it — a ratio of roughly **200:1**. Alphabet owns all four layers: silicon (TPU), data centre, model (Gemini), distribution (Search, YouTube, Android, Workspace). No competitor holds all four.

## The number that decides everything

**Roughly 60% of the programme is short-lived compute.** The assumed useful life is the single largest swing factor in reported hyperscaler earnings — 3–4 years instead of 5–6 implies materially higher depreciation. Industry-wide capex is tracking past **$700bn in 2026**; any slowdown in enterprise AI adoption leaves hundreds of billions of depreciating assets without revenue.

## What this phase establishes
1. Capex more than doubling in one year, with no guided peak.
2. Alphabet can point to a specific line where it is converting — Cloud margin expanding while growing 82%.
3. The constraint is capacity, so the spending is demand-backed rather than speculative.
4. The depreciation life assumption is the largest single unknown in this dossier.
5. Owning all four layers is the structural case for the spending being an advantage rather than a tax.

## Could not establish
- **Depreciation schedule and assumed useful life** — the biggest unknown
- Cloud backlog conversion period ($514bn over three years is a different asset from over twelve)
- TPU external revenue and margin
- Capex split between contracted cloud capacity and speculative internal training
