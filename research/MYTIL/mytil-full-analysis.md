## Phase 1 — Pricing-power architecture

The synergy claim is genuinely physical where it applies: Aluminium of Greece is one of the country's largest single electricity consumers, and Metlen owns the gas-fired generation that supplies it. **Owning both sides of a smelter's largest input cost is a real vertical hedge.** But that covers two of five sub-sectors. The link between an aluminium smelter and an EPC contractor is not a synergy — it is a conglomerate.

**There is almost no pricing power in the largest businesses.** Aluminium and alumina are LME-priced; Greek and European wholesale power is exchange-priced. Metlen is a price-taker in both, and its margin is a cost-position and hedging outcome, not a pricing decision.

**The one place real pricing power is being created is gallium.** A €295.5m FID taken in January 2025 targets 2Mt bauxite, 1.26Mt alumina and **50 tonnes of gallium a year** at Agios Nikolaos — which would make Metlen the only significant primary gallium producer in the European Union, in a market China has restricted by export control. Bauxite from 2026, alumina and gallium phasing from 2027, full scale 2028. **The thesis does not mention it.**

**The risk the thesis omits entirely is fixed-price construction.** M Power Projects takes lump-sum EPC contracts — unlimited downside, capped upside — and that risk materialised. It landed on the very segment the thesis leans on: **M Renewables profitability fell ~78% in FY2025** because the EPC losses sit inside it. The renewables business is a developer-contractor carrying construction risk, not a yield asset.

## Phase 2 — Asset base: the pipeline is inventory, not installed base

| Measure | Value | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Operational portfolio** | **1.2 GW** | The actual asset base |
| H1 2026 generation | ~0.9 TWh | What it produced |
| **Total pipeline** | **>12 GW** | Inventory to be built and sold |
| Pipeline composition | ~57% PV, ~35% storage | The two lowest-barrier technologies |
| Asset rotation target | **1.0–1.5 GW/year** | The model: build then sell |
| H1 2026 disposal | 283 MW UK solar | Completed |

**Ten parts in eleven are not built — and they are not meant to be held.** Metlen runs an explicit asset-rotation model. A 12 GW pipeline owned by an IPP is a future annuity to be discounted; a 12 GW pipeline owned by a developer on asset rotation is a stream of **development margins and construction contracts** — cyclical, execution-dependent, worth a materially lower multiple.

The composition compounds it: PV and storage are in global oversupply with no component bottleneck. **This is the exact inverse of Siemens Energy's transformer position.**

*The group's best asset is the one the thesis mentions least, and the one it leads with has the weakest barrier.*

**Reverse valuation.** At ~7.7× FY2026E EBITDA the price implies roughly no growth from here and a permanent conglomerate discount. Given a funded gallium and alumina expansion reaching full scale in 2028 and a structural change to the shareholder base, that is a demanding thing to assume.

**And the upside does not require the medium-term target.** €1.5bn of EBITDA rather than the targeted €2bn, at 8.5×, is roughly **€78 per share**. The thesis can be substantially wrong and the investment can still work.

### Three non-consensus points

1. **"Record profitability" skips the year in between.** FY2025 revenue +25%, EBITDA −30%, net profit −49%, on Protos EPC losses that cut M Renewables profitability ~78%. FY2026 guidance merely returns EBITDA to the FY2024 level. Three-year growth: approximately zero.
2. **The 12 GW pipeline is inventory, not an asset base.** 1.2 GW operational; explicit build-and-sell at 1.0–1.5 GW/yr; ~57% PV and ~35% storage, both in global oversupply. Development margins deserve a development multiple, not an annuity multiple.
3. **The emphasis is inverted.** Aluminium of Greece — the EU's largest integrated bauxite-alumina-aluminium operation, in a bloc not permitting new primary smelters — is the only asset here that cannot be rebuilt. And the funded expansion adds 50 t/yr of **gallium** from 2027, the EU's only significant primary source of a metal China controls by export licence. That is where the real pricing power is being created, and the thesis does not mention it.

### What the bulls are correctly confident about

1. **One real reason for the discount has been deliberately removed.** A FTSE 100 constituent with a primary London listing has a structurally different buyer base from a Greek mid-cap, and index flows do not care about conglomerate logic. This is mechanical, not speculative — the strongest argument for the bull case.
2. **The deleveraging is real and fast.** €728m in six months, leverage halved from 3.1× to 1.7×. Companies converting profit into balance sheet at that rate are usually not value traps. Cash is the hardest fact in any dossier, and this one has it.
3. **There is enormous room for error in the assumptions.** €1.5bn of EBITDA instead of €2bn still gives ~€78/share. That is rare, and it is the best argument against a flat HOLD.

### Trigger-to-sell
- Any further lump-sum EPC loss disclosure in M Power Projects
- Net leverage returning above 2.5×
- FY2026 EBITDA below €1.0bn — the bottom of guidance
- M Renewables profitability failing to recover to pre-2025 levels in FY2026
- Delay to gallium or alumina commissioning beyond 2028
- Asset rotation delivery below 1.0 GW in a full year
- Aluminium prices falling >20% without a corresponding fall in group power costs

### Re-entry test
Below **~€42** the shares offer a base-case IRR above 18% with the sum-of-the-parts providing a floor — that justifies a full position. At €46.62 the 11% upside is **inside the error bars** on derived net debt and estimated segment EBITDA, so a starter position at most, and patience for a better entry. The evidentiary test is simpler than a price: **a full year of the lump-sum EPC book completing without further losses, plus a disclosed segment split confirming the sum-of-the-parts.** Both arrive with the FY2026 results.

### Largest gaps
1. **Remaining lump-sum EPC exposure after Protos** — the FY2025 loss cut group EBITDA 30%; whether it can recur is undisclosed and is the most important unknown here.
2. **Segment EBITDA split** — the sum-of-the-parts carries 40% weight and rests entirely on an estimate.
3. **The bridge from FY2026 guidance to the €2bn target** — ~€900m of EBITDA with no public allocation.
4. **Reported net debt and share count** — together comparable in size to the 11% upside.
