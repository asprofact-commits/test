## The two businesses inside one share

| | Income portfolio | The Ellinikon |
|---|---|---|
| Assets | 4 shopping centres + Flisvos Marina | 6.2m sqm former Athens airport site |
| Q1 2026 EBITDA | **€29.4m (record)** | Negative — consumes capital |
| Pricing power | Dominant prime retail, little competing supply | Irreplaceable site; **~€14,000/sqm** at Riviera Tower |
| Cash character | Generates | Consumes capital, receives pre-sale proceeds |
| Risk | Consumer, cap rates | Construction cost, delivery, absorption |

**The mature assets fund the development, and the arithmetic shows it starkly.** Malls (€24.9m, +5%, record) plus marina (€4.5m, +12%, record) produced €29.4m in Q1 — while *group* EBITDA before valuations was only **€12.0–12.3m**. Ellinikon and corporate consumed ~€17m in the quarter. The group posted a **€17.7m net loss on record revenue of €143m, up 35%**.

Revenue up a third and a loss at the bottom is what the heaviest part of a development cycle looks like.

## Phase 3 — The finding: the third instance of one mechanism

**Cumulative Ellinikon proceeds exceeded €1.7bn through May 2026, against cumulative investment of ~€1.1bn through March.** The project has taken in more cash than it has spent.

That is not a profitable project. It is **customers paying in advance for homes that have not been built.**

| Company | Cash received ahead of | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| GE Vernova | Turbine delivery | FCF ~2.0× adjusted EBITDA |
| Siemens Energy | Equipment delivery | FCF ~1.5× profit before special items |
| **LAMDA Development** | **Home construction** | **€1.7bn proceeds vs €1.1bn spend** |

Three companies, three sectors, **one structure**: cash in advance of an undischarged obligation. In every case the cash is real and it is not the same thing as profit.

The practical consequence: **€831m of group cash is not all free.** An unquantified portion is customer money against undelivered units, and the construction cost of delivering them is a future call on it. Management expects ~€600m of residential inflows in 2026 against ~€500m of Ellinikon capex — close to self-funding on cash while loss-making on accounting. *Both statements describe the same mechanism.*

## Phase 4 — The reconciliation this dossier cannot complete

The debt is disclosed with unusual precision: **€1,470m of borrowings** (€815m holdco / €655m malls) at an average cost of **3.8%** — genuinely cheap for a Greek developer — with a €500m bond running to 2032. Against €831m of cash, net debt is ~**€639m**. Modest gearing for a developer.

Then:

| Step | €m |
|---|---|
| Stated investment portfolio | >3,800 |
| Less net debt | −639 |
| Would imply NAV of | ~3,161 |
| **Published NAV** | **~1,533** |
| **UNEXPLAINED** | **~1,600** |

**Roughly €1.6bn of claims sit between the portfolio value and shareholders' net asset value.** Customer contract liabilities, remaining consideration owed to the Greek state for the Ellinikon site, minorities and deferred tax are the plausible candidates — none identifiable from available disclosure.

This does **not** invalidate the published NAV, which is independently valued and is the primary anchor. It does mean **the €3.8bn portfolio figure must never be used per share** — and it is used nowhere in this valuation.

One near-term item: a **€320m bond at 3.4% matures in 2027** and refinances into a higher-rate market (the 2032 issue priced at 3.8%).

**Reverse valuation.** At €6.24 the price implies a 30% discount to NAV. To justify *no* discount, the market would need to believe the programme carries no delivery risk. To justify a 45% discount, that The Ellinikon is worth materially less than its invested capital. The truth is very likely between — which is why the honest answer here is a narrow one.

### Why HOLD, when the model says +8%

The model returns roughly +8%, and the memo does not overrule the arithmetic — **it overrules the conclusion someone might draw from it.** An 8% gap on a company whose €3.8bn stated portfolio and €1,533m NAV differ by €1.6bn of claims I cannot identify is not a margin of safety; it is a rounding error inside an unresolved reconciliation. The rating moves to a buy on one disclosure, named below.

### Three non-consensus points

1. **The Ellinikon's cash generation is pre-sale money, not profit.** €1.7bn of proceeds against €1.1bn of spend, with the construction obligation still outstanding — structurally identical to GE Vernova's and Siemens Energy's customer advances. €831m of cash is not all free.
2. **The €3.8bn portfolio figure is not attributable to shareholders.** Net debt explains only €639m of the €2.27bn gap to NAV. Never use it per share.
3. **This may not be an Ellinikon story at all.** At a 7% cap rate the malls and marina alone support ~€6.03/share against a €6.24 price. The interesting question isn't whether The Ellinikon is valuable — it's why the market has decided it's worth roughly zero, and whether a €1.6bn unexplained equity bridge is part of the answer.

### What the bulls are correctly confident about

1. **The site cannot be replicated.** A 6.2m sqm coastal parcel inside Athens from a privatisation that won't recur, clearing at ~€14,000/sqm, with ~1,000 homes sold — real money from real buyers, not a projection.
2. **The funding engine works.** Malls +5% to a record, marina +12%, borrowing at 3.8% with a long maturity profile. That is what lets the development proceed **without an equity call** — a real distinction from PPC (+62% shares) and ADMIE Holding (+56%) in 2026.
3. **Thirteen analysts average ~€11.19** and this sits 40% below. Same pattern as all seven energy dossiers; same two honest readings — a real edge from refusing to capitalise speculative absorption, or a systematic bias in a protocol built to resist narrative. The base rate favours the analysts.

### Trigger-to-sell
- Riviera Tower delivery slipping beyond H1 2027
- Absorption on the next 300–350 units materially below the pace of the first 1,000
- Any downward revision to published NAV per share
- Malls EBITDA declining YoY for two consecutive quarters
- Greek prime retail cap rates widening beyond 8%
- The €320m 2027 bond refinanced above a 5.5% coupon
- **Any equity raise** — self-funding is central to the case
- Customer contract liabilities disclosed materially above €800m

### Re-entry test
Below **~€5.40** the shares sit under the income portfolio's value *even at an 8% cap rate* — meaning The Ellinikon would be valued at less than zero. That is a full position regardless of the reconciliation. Between €5.40 and €6.24, hold.

The evidentiary test is a document, not a price: **the balance sheet reconciling €3.8bn of portfolio value to €1,533m of NAV, plus the independent cap rates on the malls.** Those two disclosures convert this from an interesting discount into a measurable one. They arrive with the H1 2026 report.

### Largest gaps
1. **The €3.8bn → €1,533m bridge** — ~€1.6bn unidentified. The reason this is not a buy.
2. **Independent cap rates on the four malls** — the floor rests on an assumed 7%; a 150bp range is worth €2.00/share.
3. **Customer contract liabilities inside the €831m cash balance.**
4. **Remaining construction cost against homes already sold.**
5. **H1 2026 results** — only Q1 was available, so this dossier is a quarter stale.
