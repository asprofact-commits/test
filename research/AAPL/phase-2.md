# Apple — Phase 2: Asset base & physical bottlenecks

**Apple Inc.** · NASDAQ: AAPL · ~$328/share (derived: $4.79tn market cap ÷ 14.59bn shares) · 2026-09-05

> **Evidence note.** No primary filing was retrievable — every external domain is blocked by this environment's egress proxy. Findings rest on search-result summaries of Apple's 8-K/10-Q releases and reputable press. Labels record what the source claimed, not independent verification.

## An asset-light base by design

Capex was **~$4bn across H1 FY2026** against **$83bn of operating cash flow** — under 5%. Apple owns almost no manufacturing; its differentiating asset is silicon and software integration, which **does not appear on the balance sheet**.

That has a direct methodological consequence: **price-to-book and conventional ROIC are not meaningful here**, and Phase 5 excludes them rather than reporting a misleadingly high multiple.

## The opposite side of the AI capex trade

| | Apple | Hyperscaler cohort |
|---|---|---|
| AI capability | **Rented** — ~$1bn/yr to Google for Gemini | Built — tens of billions of capex |
| H1 FY2026 capex | ~$4bn | Vastly higher |

Apple is buying frontier AI at roughly **0.25%** of what the hyperscalers are committing. This is either exceptional capital discipline or strategic dependence — Phase 3 decides which. The uncomfortable detail: the supplier is simultaneously Apple's **largest revenue counterparty, antitrust co-defendant, and a direct competitor**.

## Cost pressures Apple is choosing to absorb

| Pressure | Magnitude | Response |
|---|---|---|
| US tariffs | ~$900m/quarter | Absorbing; ~2pp refund benefit in Q3 will not recur |
| India production | **5–8% above China** | Accepting, for geopolitical diversification |
| Memory/component crunch | Industry-wide | Absorbing to hold price and take share |
| FX | Cited in Q4 guidance | Unhedged residual |

Every row is a cost being absorbed rather than passed on. It shows up in the guided Q4 gross margin step-down: **50.1% → 47–48%**.

## The bottleneck was supply, not demand

Management cited **supply constraints** in Q4 guidance while iPhone grew 22% for two consecutive quarters. An unusually good problem — and one that flatters nothing in the valuation.

## What this phase establishes
1. Asset intensity is minimal; the key asset is unrecorded → exclude book-based methods.
2. Apple has taken the opposite side of the AI capex trade. This is the central strategic bet.
3. The binding constraint in FY2026 was supply.
4. Gross margin quality is lower than the headline — ~2pp of Q3's 50.1% was non-recurring.
