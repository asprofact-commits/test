# Apple — Phase 1: Pricing-power architecture

**Apple Inc.** · NASDAQ: AAPL · ~$328/share (derived: $4.79tn market cap ÷ 14.59bn shares) · 2026-09-05

> **Evidence note.** No primary filing was retrievable — every external domain is blocked by this environment's egress proxy. Findings rest on search-result summaries of Apple's 8-K/10-Q releases and reputable press. Labels record what the source claimed, not independent verification.

## Who sets the price

Apple does. There is no regulator, no tariff, no rate case — the inverse of the KEPCO case. Pricing power rests on brand, switching costs and an installed base above **2.5bn active devices** (+150m YoY) with **>1.5bn paid subscriptions**.

It is demonstrated, not asserted. In China — the hardest market in the world — Apple **held prices while competitors raised theirs**, and took share from **13.9% to 18.1%** between Q2 2025 and Q2 2026, growing ~25% in a market that fell 4.3%. Huawei holds 22.6%. Company gross margin hit **50.1%** in Q3 FY2026.

## The real vulnerability: one counterparty, not one regulator

Google pays Apple an estimated **$20bn a year** for default search placement — 36% of Safari search ad revenue, and close to pure margin. That is roughly **13% of estimated FY2026 operating income**.

Judge Mehta's remedy let the payments continue but stripped their protection:
- no longer **exclusive**
- Google **cannot prevent Apple promoting rivals** (search or generative AI)
- revenue share **cannot be conditioned on default status beyond 12 months**

Both sides are appealing — Google on liability (May 2026), the DOJ on remedy (Feb 2026). So ~13% of operating income, at near-100% margin, has moved from a locked contract to an **annual renegotiation with an unresolved legal overhang**. Apple's position may even improve — it can now auction what it previously had to grant — but it is repriced every year.

## Regulatory erosion at the edges

The EU fined Apple €500m in April 2025 under the DMA; on 18 August 2026 the Commission signalled no periodic fines, closing the dispute. From **1 October 2026** EU commissions become tiered: **26%** (Apple IAP), **20%** (alternative payment), **15%** (small business, mini-apps, second-year subscriptions). Commentary calls it Apple "conceding very little" — the in-app take rate falls 30% → 26%, not disintermediation.

## Governance

No controlling shareholder, no state ownership, no owner whose objectives conflict with minorities. Capital returns are limited only by cash generation.

## What this phase establishes
1. Pricing power is real and was just proven in the hardest market available.
2. The vulnerability is counterparty concentration, not regulation: ~$20bn of near-costless profit now on a 12-month non-exclusive contract.
3. EU pressure settled on terms that are directionally negative but quantitatively minor.
4. Apple is **absorbing** cost inflation (tariffs ~$900m/qtr, India +5–8%, memory crunch) to defend share — it lands on gross margin.
5. Governance is the mirror image of a state-controlled monopoly.

## Could not establish
- Exact terms of the renegotiated Google agreement (**the largest single swing factor**)
- EU as a share of App Store revenue — without it the 30→26% change cannot be sized
- Whether the ~2pp tariff-refund benefit in Q3 gross margin recurs (Q4 guidance says no)

## What it changes
The bear case on Apple **cannot** be a pricing-power case. It has to be about concentration of high-margin profit in one annually-repriced contract, and about valuation.
