# Apple — Phase 4: Capital structure & returns on capital

**Apple Inc.** · NASDAQ: AAPL · ~$328/share (derived: $4.79tn market cap ÷ 14.59bn shares) · 2026-09-05

> **Evidence note.** No primary filing was retrievable — every external domain is blocked by this environment's egress proxy. Findings rest on search-result summaries of Apple's 8-K/10-Q releases and reputable press. Labels record what the source claimed, not independent verification.

## There is no balance-sheet question

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Total debt | ~$85bn |
| Cash & investments | ~$147bn |
| **Net cash** | **~$62bn** |
| FY2026E free cash flow | ~$135bn (~28% of revenue) |
| Buyback authorised Apr-2026 | $100bn |
| Dividend | $0.27/qtr → ~0.33% yield |

No covenant, rating trigger or statutory ceiling binds. Apple could fund any plausible programme from cash on hand. **Returns on capital exceed the cost of capital by an extraordinary margin** — the exact inverse of the KEPCO case. The precise ROIC ratio is not economically meaningful because a decade of buybacks has shrunk book equity to a fraction of the economic asset base.

## Mandatory sensitivities — and why the usual ones don't matter

| Shock | Effect | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Rates **+100bp** | **+$0.6bn** pre-tax | Net interest on net cash — Apple *benefits* |
| Rates **+200bp** | **+$1.2bn** pre-tax | As above |
| **USD +10%** | **−$28bn revenue, −$13bn gross profit** | ~58% international, before hedging |
| Tariffs | ~−$900m/quarter | Being absorbed |
| **Loss of Google payment** | **−$20bn operating income** | Near-100% margin, unreplaceable |

The ranking *is* the finding: currency and one contract matter enormously; leverage does not matter at all.

## The buyback is a weaker support than it looks

$100bn/yr is ~2% of market cap. At **37x earnings** it retires far less value per dollar than the same programme did at 15x. Capital return does not rescue a valuation; it is levered to it.

## What this phase establishes
1. No balance-sheet risk, no funding risk, no returns problem.
2. Rate sensitivity is trivial and favourable; **currency is the real exposure**.
3. The buyback's value-creation is weakened by the multiple it is executed at.
4. With leverage and returns eliminated, **the entire case reduces to valuation**.

## Could not establish

## What it changes
Phase 4 removes every risk that normally decides an industrial case and makes Phase 5 unusually load-bearing. The answer rests on discount rate and terminal assumptions — exactly where a DCF is weakest, which is why the **reverse DCF** is the more honest instrument.
